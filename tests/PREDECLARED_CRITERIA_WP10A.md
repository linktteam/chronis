### 3.3 The three new metrics (each is computed on the 14-day windows after the first fit, replayed through the production lineage at the chosen operating point)
- **(a) Names agree when both seeds serve.** Case = a 14-day window (t = 104, 118, 132, 146, 150) at which both the seed-A and the seed-B lineage end `ALIGNED`; agree = the per-day stable regime ids over the window agree on at least 99% of days (WP9 definition). **Target >= 98%** (the owner's level; reason: it separates a naming failure from a serve-versus-withhold disagreement). Verdict by the wording of section 2; to be MET the Wilson lower bound must be >= 98%, which needs at least 188 cases with no disagreement, so the pooled 108-user set is the headline.
- **(b) Serve/withhold agreement between seeds.** Case = a 14-day window (as above) at which at least one seed has an accepted fit; agree = both seeds end `ALIGNED` or both end not `ALIGNED`. **No target** until the dev numbers are seen; after the development readout I declare one for WP10B in a dated section (the brief asks for this), and the evaluation judges it only as "reported". Also reported: both-serve / both-withhold / exactly-one-serves counts, and the same agreement restricted to windows where either seed serves.
- **(c) Days to first served lineage** (the product-relevant number). Seed-A weekly chain; the first fit is at t = 90 and names the lineage; a lineage is **served** at the first later window whose step ends `ALIGNED`; days to first served = t* - 90 (7 to 60; users with no `ALIGNED` step by t = 150 are censored at 60 and counted separately). Reported per stratum and pooled: share served by day 14, 28 and 60 after the first fit, median days among served (and with censored users counted as 61), quartiles, number never served. Same for seed B on its 14-day schedule. No target.
- **Old criterion beside them:** strict identical lineage among served (WP9 A1-1, target 95%, definition unchanged), reported as in WP9.

### 3.4 Evaluation (run once, at the chosen operating point)
- Judged: coverage >= 80% and wrong-and-unflagged <= 1% (the WP9 A2 levels, **not moved**) pooled and **per stratum** with Wilson 95% intervals; (a), (b), (c), the old criterion; the secondary flag-OFF line and the line at the current default (h = 2.5, flag ON), both replayed on the same fits, reported without a verdict.
- If 80% and 1% are not jointly reached, the curve (coverage against wrong-and-unflagged over the six candidates) is shown again and the report says which stratum drives it. A stratum that meets both is named.
- **Safety demonstration (the 1% claim).** Unit = a gate-eligible alignment (an accepted refit except the first fit of a chain; WP9 denominator), seed A, weekly chain, 10 restarts, at the chosen operating point, over the 108 users. **The 1% bound is demonstrated for a stratum when there are zero wrong-and-unflagged alignments and the one-sided exact 95% upper bound `1 - 0.05^(1/n)` is at most 1% (n >= 299; the rule of three of the brief).** The two-sided Wilson 95% upper bound is reported beside it. With 36 users per stratum and 9 alignments per user, n is 324 if every fit is accepted; if fewer than 299 alignments exist in a stratum the report gives the achievable bound and says it is not demonstrated. One wrong alignment in a stratum means not demonstrated for that stratum (with one wrong, n >= 473 is needed).
- **What would count as failure:** any wrong-and-unflagged alignment at the chosen point in the evaluation population; names-agree below 98% on the point estimate; coverage below 80% in a stratum (expected for hard: stated in WP9 section 9 and not changed); no stratum meeting both levels.
- **Declared expectation (cannot be moved):** hard fails coverage, as in WP9; the gate in 2.0..2.5 will not by itself reach 80% pooled; wrong-and-unflagged stays at 0 for the flag-ON candidates; the names-agree point estimate is close to 98% and cannot be demonstrated unless at least 188 cases show no disagreement.

# Part 2 (dated 2026-11-26, before any A4 fit): A4, a negative control that can fail (three designs, development seeds only, one evaluation)

## 5. What I had seen
- READ: `tests/WP9_A4_RESULTS.md` and WP9 criteria Part 6: the control Z (each fit compared in its own z-space instead of through the anchor's stored scaler) was **inert** in WP8 (growing windows from day 0) and in WP9 (three designs of noise-scale changes on fresh 45-day windows; 0 wrong-and-unflagged for R and Z, 0 paired differences in 112 alignments). The WP9 reading (not tested): the window mean and sd that own-z-scoring uses are dominated by the **between-regime spread**, not by the within-regime noise that was scaled; so only a change that moves the window's own origin or unit (regime occupancy, regime means) can separate Z from R.
- READ: `backbone/hssm/lineage.py`: the matching is a Hungarian assignment on regime means in raw units divided by the anchor's stored scaler; the anchor moves to the new fit on `ALIGNED` and `NEW_LINEAGE` (so a chain compares each fit with the last served one); `patch_zspace` of `a4_analyze.py` makes each summary use its own scaler (the Z control).
- I have **not** fitted any A4 seed of this package. The three A3 development fits being generated now (users 10001..10048) are production-protocol chains and I have not read any alignment result from them.

## 6. The three designs (each with the reason it should break Z; all on the easy stratum, K = 3, 150-day streams, 10 restarts, production selection and publish rule, lineage defaults of this commit, h = 2.5, flag ON)
- **D, a scale change correlated with a regime-mean change.** Protocol S of WP9 (fresh 45-day windows ending at 45, 60, ..., 150; chain; the first window is the anchor). Generator option `regime_scale_change = dict(regime = 2, features = (0, 1, 2, 3), factor = 3.0, start = 60)` (new, additive, default off, default streams pinned bit-identical): from day 60, on the days of planted regime 2 only, the regime's mean offset from the regime-average mean and its within-regime noise on those features are tripled. **Reason:** the raw-unit identity of the regimes stays recoverable (regime 2 moves further out in its own direction), but the window's own mean and sd on four features change with the share of regime-2 days in the window, so the z-position of regimes 0 and 1 relative to the origin and to each other moves, while the stored scaler keeps the raw geometry. The change is not uniform across features (four of seven) and not uniform across regimes (one of three), which a monotone rescaling is not.
- **E, windows of very different length.** No planted change. Anchor window [0, 90); each later window is **short**: [t - L, t) for t in 105, 120, 135, 150 and L in 20, 30 (eight windows per user); each short window is aligned with the long anchor as its own one-pair chain (so the anchor is always the long window). **Reason:** the own z-space of a short window has its origin at that window's mean and its unit at that window's sd; with dwell times of 5 to 30 days a 20-day window is dominated by one or two regimes, so its mean sits inside one regime and its sd is small, which moves every z-mean by a different amount in each feature; the anchor's stored scaler is the long window's, so R compares raw means in the anchor's units and does not care how short the new window is.
- **F, a planted fault in the production path on the production protocol.** The production growing windows [0, t) of the A3 users (the A3 fits: seed A weekly chains of 10 restarts, replayed twice, with and without the fault); the fault = the stored scaler replaced by the per-window scaler inside the matching (`patch_zspace`; the same replacement as the WP8/WP9 control, here on the production windows). **Reason (brief):** if this fault does not produce wrong alignments on the production protocol, the stored scaler is **not** what protects the identity there; this is the direct statement of the claim, at no new fit cost (replay only). I expect it to be inert (WP8), and say so now.

## 9. Protocol (the WP9 protocol of `wp9/scripts/b5_run.py`, unchanged except the new seeds and the extra output)
180-day streams of the default generator (K = 3, separation 1.0 to 2.5), baseline K = 3 model fitted by the production `fit_hssm` on days [0, 90) (10 restarts, 60 iterations, fit seed = user) and frozen; the held-out one-step score of every day 90..179 on three streams that are identical before day 135: **none**, **regime shift** (every regime mean moved by 2.0 within-regime sd along a fixed random direction from day 135; direction seed [user, 99]), **sensor change** (noise of features 0, 1, 2 times 4 from day 135). A user whose fit raises is excluded and counted; a fit the publish rule withholds is kept (the monitor does not need a published fit; WP9 did the same).

## 10. The three candidate monitors (declared now; none has been run; parameters are frozen here and are not tuned)
All keep window 14, reference 28, cooldown 14, `min_drop_sd` 0.75 and `drop_z` 3.5 of the production monitor, and its event rule (drop >= 0.75 s0 and drop / se >= 3.5), and differ in the series or in the standard error `se`:
- **MA, block-bootstrap standard error.** Same series (S1). `se` is the standard deviation of `ref* - recent*` over B = 2000 draws, where `recent*` is the mean of 2 blocks of 7 consecutive scores and `ref*` the mean of 4 such blocks, all drawn with replacement from the blocks (any start inside the 28-day reference) of the reference window; `np.random.default_rng(0)` per decision day. Reason: the blocks carry the clustering that the AR(1) inflation does not.
- **MB, transition-excluded score.** The production rule on the series with the days removed on which the frozen model's decoded regime (argmax of the filtered posterior at that day) differs from that of the day before, and the day after such a day (the filter lags by about one day). Reason: the clustering comes from the transition days; a regime shift in the means or a sensor change does not create transitions.
- **MC, regime-standardised residual.** For each regime k the mean `m_k` and sd `s_k` of the one-step scores of the TRAINING days 0..89 on which the decoded regime is k (a regime with fewer than 5 such days uses the pooled mean and sd); the series is `e_t = (score_t - m_{k_t}) / s_{k_t}` with `k_t` the decoded regime of day t; the production rule on `e_t`. Reason: a score that is low because the day sits in a low-scoring regime stops being a drop.
**M0** = the production monitor on S1, run on the same users and reported beside the candidates (not a candidate).

## 11. Development rule and evaluation (frozen by this commit)
- **Levels (WP9, not moved):** false alarm on the none streams <= 10% of users; detection of the regime shift >= 70%; detection of the sensor change >= 70%; a user is "alarmed" in the none stream when any event occurs on any scoreable day, "detected" in a change stream when an event occurs on or after day 135 (as `b5_analyze.py` of WP9). Verdicts as in WP9: MET when the Wilson 95% bound is on the right side of the level; "MET on the point estimate, NOT DEMONSTRATED" when only the point estimate is; NOT MET otherwise.
- **Choice on the 48 development users:** a candidate is *eligible* if its point estimates meet all three levels; among eligible candidates take the lowest false-alarm rate (ties: the higher regime-shift detection, then the order MA, MB, MC). If none is eligible, take the candidate with the smallest total shortfall `max(0, FA - 0.10) + max(0, 0.70 - det_shift) + max(0, 0.70 - det_sensor)` (ties: the order MA, MB, MC) and say so.

The run is frozen in `tests/DESIGN_FREEZE_WP10A_A5.txt` (`{"monitor": "MA"|"MB"|"MC"}`) and the evaluation script refuses to start without it.

# Part 4b (dated 2026-11-26, before any A5 fit; before any A5 seed was generated): candidate MA is redefined after a check on pure noise and on a toy clustered series
- **What I did (no A5 seed involved):** to test the code of `a5_monitors.py` I ran candidate MA exactly as declared in section 10 on 200 series of iid N(0, 1) noise of 120 days. **False alarm 16% (MA) against 2% for the production monitor on the same series.** Reason (READ from the arithmetic, not tested further): the bootstrap standard error estimated from 22 overlapping block means of a 28-day reference is biased low and heavy-tailed, so |z| >= 3.5 occurs far too often.
- **The change:** MA's standard error is the **larger** of the production AR(1)-inflated standard error and the block-bootstrap one (everything else as declared). It can only make the rule more conservative than the production monitor; on 300 iid series of 120 days its false alarm was 1.7%. A property that follows and is tested: the first event of MA is never earlier than the first event of the production monitor on the same series.
- **A toy check of the clustering claim (also no A5 seed):** series of regime-like levels (dwell 5 to 24 days, level sd 0.7, within-level sd 0.6, 120 days), 300 series: false alarm of the production monitor 67%, of MA as first declared 77%, of the redefined MA 64%; with a 56-day reference 39% (not adopted: a longer reference changes the horizon of the study, and the false-alarm exposure with it, so the comparison with the WP9 levels would not be like for like). **Reading:** a variance estimated from a 28-day reference cannot see the between-regime variance, so I expect the redefined MA to improve little; MB and MC remove the regime component and are the candidates I expect to matter. This is stated now so that it cannot be moved.
- Nothing else in Part 4 changes: the choice rule, the levels, the seeds (10401..10546), the protocol and the evaluation are as declared there.
