## What was implemented, and why (dual model only; the baseline and every default are unchanged)
* **R1 `collapsed_regime` for the dual model.** A regime's effective days are the sum over rows of the FILTERED `p_t_posterior[:, k]` (the posterior the result already carries). A fit is collapsed iff the minimum over regimes is strictly below `COLLAPSE_MIN_EFFECTIVE_DAYS` = 4.0 (the baseline rule and constant; the 4-day rule is not changed). It fills the existing `fit_collapsed` / `collapse_report` and so the existing `collapsed_regime:` withheld reason. Why: before this item a dual fit with an empty regime was served as healthy.
* **R2 `degenerate_duration`.** A regime is degenerate if its duration parameters sit at a wall of the box or its duration pmf is still rising at the last lattice step (`pmf[Dmax] > pmf[Dmax-1]`, no threshold). New fields `fit_duration_degenerate`, `duration_degenerate_report`; new withheld reason `degenerate_duration: ...`. Why: Item 1 re-verified that segments longer than Dmax go with degenerate durations (S2: Fisher p = 0.0009, odds ratio 29.75), and a duration law truncated at the lattice end is not a trustworthy fit.
* **R3 opt-in `max_duration_retry`** (`fit_hssm` keyword, default `None` = nothing changes). Dual model without a shared prior only; an integer larger than `max_duration`. If the selected fit is degenerate by R2, the selected K is refitted once with the larger Dmax and the retry replaces the first fit only if it is NOT degenerate; `convergence_metadata["dmax_retry"]` records `requested`, `from`, `first_fit_degenerate`, `adopted`. Why opt-in: a larger Dmax changes the model class and costs time; Item 1 supports it (Dmax = 60 cured 8 of 9) but on development-size evidence.
* **Not built: Item 5c, the dual publish gate** (and so there is no "share of fits that pass the gate" to report). Item 1 showed no consistent trend of the held-out score with more EM, which is the brief's condition for skipping it.
* No numeric output of any fit changes: only whether a dual fit is publishable (and, with R3 used, which Dmax was fitted).

## How often the rules fire (RUN, descriptive)
* Plain dual path, 12 users, `max_iter` 200: R2 flags 4 of 12 (all non-converged anyway), R1 none.
* Shrinkage path, same 12 users: R2 flags 4 of 12, including 1 of the 7 fits that were publishable.
* Item 3 stage B (24 users x 3 lengths, shrinkage, `max_iter` 200): of the 38 previously publishable `shrink5` fits, **16 are newly withheld** (R2 on 14, R1 on 4, some on both) plus the 2 publishable `personal` fits; see `tests/WP7R_ITEM3_RESULTS.md` for what that does to the cold-start verdict. Item 4 (12 users, `max_iter` 200): R2 withheld 1 of 12 shrinkage fits although it was `CONVERGED` (its coverage there was 0.92, so that fit was not badly calibrated; one fit).
* So on prior-anchored fits the new rules are not rare: they remove 43%, 31% and 55% of the `shrink5` fits that the old rules published at lengths 30, 60 and 120 in the Item 3 design (6 of 14, 4 of 13, 6 of 11). Whether R2's "pmf still rising at the last lattice step" is too strict for short series (where a still-rising pmf may simply reflect few segments) is not tested here; the owner may want to look at that before the rule goes into production.
