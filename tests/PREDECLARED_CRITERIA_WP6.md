## 2. Item 1 (input guard and contract): declared limits

* **`MAX_TIMESTAMP_SPAN_DAYS = 20000`** (new constant in `backbone/hssm/config.py`). Reason: 20,000 days is 54.8 years, longer than any person's record; a record of seconds exceeds it after 5.5 hours; milliseconds after 20 seconds. * Unchanged: results for every input the old code accepted and that satisfies the checks (bit-identical), the cold-start gate and its order (the timestamp check runs first, so a malformed argument raises `ValueError` even when the record is also too short).
* Tests: one per check with its negative control (the valid neighbour passes and gives the same fit as before); boundary at exactly the limit passes and limit + 1 day fails; the owner's case (seconds) raises before any lattice is built (checked by patching `np.full` to fail if called with a huge shape). No engine change in Item 2. The diagnostic variants live in scripts under `integration_report/wp6/scripts/`; an instrumented copy of the EM loop is accepted only if it reproduces the library's `fit_state`, `n_iter` and log-likelihood history bit for bit (checked on every fit).

**Q1. `REJECTED_UPDATE` (rate, size, cause).** Per family and model: rate per restart; the decrease `prev_ll - ll` at the guard; the iteration of the event; words: **noise** if the decrease is at most 1e-6 nats (about 1e-9 relative at these likelihoods, floating point level), **small** if at most 1e-3, **large** otherwise. Cause by block: at each event compute the change of each block of the expected complete-data log-likelihood Q under the E-step statistics of the previous parameters (emissions, durations, transition matrix, initial distribution; for the dual model also the two continuous blocks). EM theory: if every block's update did not lower its Q, the likelihood cannot drop; so a drop means at least one block has `dQ_b < 0`, and the blocks with `dQ_b < 0` are the cause. If all `dQ_b >= 0` and the likelihood still dropped, the E-step approximation is the cause (possible for the dual model: GPB2).
**Q2. Collapse.** A baseline fit is *collapsed* if any variance is at the floor (`var <= 1.001e-3`) or any regime has fewer than 4 effective days (`sum_t regime_posterior[t, k] < 4`, the brief's number). Per family: the share collapsed, by final state; the log-likelihood of collapsed against the best non-collapsed restart of the same dataset; the number of (regime, feature) pairs at the floor and the days the regime holds; the log-likelihood gain explained by the floor, computed as the sum over the regime's days and its floored features of `log N(x; mu, 1e-3) - log N(x; mu, v)` with `v` the median variance of the same feature in the non-collapsed regimes (so the answer to "why higher" is a number, not a story).
**Q3. Dual duration degeneracy.** A duration is *degenerate* if `dur_sigma >= 3` or `exp(dur_mu) > 10 * Dmax` (a log-normal with sigma 3 has a coefficient of variation of about 90; a typical dwell beyond ten lattice lengths is outside what the lattice can describe; both are far from the healthy range seen so far, sigma of about 0.3 to 1.4). For each degenerate regime: the entry mass `N_{k,d}` (share in the last three cells), the fitted pmf on 1..Dmax, the power-law exponent `a = mu / sigma^2 - 1` of the large-parameter limit, the KL divergence from that pmf to the best pmf with sigma at most 2 and mu in [-5, 8] (if tiny, the parameters are a reparametrisation of an ordinary pmf: a ridge), the Nelder-Mead success flag and iteration count, and the number of entries in the regime. For seed 6010 specifically the same table plus the trajectory of (mu, sigma) over iterations.
**Q4. Stopping.** Rates of `CONVERGED`, `MAX_ITER_REACHED`, `REJECTED_UPDATE` at `n_iter` = 200 (baseline and dual). *Still rising*: the log-likelihood gains more than 0.01 nats over the last 10 iterations. *Premature convergence* (N2): for fits that ended `CONVERGED`, continue the EM loop for 50 more iterations from the final parameters with the stopping rules off; the fit was premature if the log-likelihood rises by more than 0.01 nats (100 times the tolerance 1e-4) in that continuation. Report both rates.
**Q5. Candidate fixes** for each cause, with their risks, in `tests/WP6_CONVERGENCE_DIAGNOSIS.md`; the document is committed before any fix is written.

**What each statement of the document may say (rules written now).** *"The decrease is numerical noise"* only if at least 90% of the events are at most 1e-6 nats. *"The decrease is the M-step"* for the block(s) with `dQ_b < 0` in at least half of the events. *"The floor explains the higher log-likelihood"* only if the explained gain covers at least 80% of the gap between the collapsed fit and the best non-collapsed restart of the dataset. *"The duration degeneracy is a ridge"* only if the KL divergence to the nearest ordinary pmf is below 1e-3 nats for at least half of the degenerate regimes. *"Convergence is premature"* only if the premature rate among `CONVERGED` fits is above 5%. When a rule does not fire, the document says "not shown" and gives the numbers; a cause is named UNKNOWN if the numbers do not support one.

## 4. Item 3, part 1 (baseline repair, collapse, multi-start metadata, P0-07). Dated 2026-11-01, after the Item 2 diagnosis (7d754be), BEFORE any engine change

What I have seen since section 3: the diagnosis document `tests/WP6_CONVERGENCE_DIAGNOSIS.md` (baseline Q1, Q2, Q2b, Q4; dual Q1, Q3, Q4). I have NOT seen dual continuation results (Q4b) yet: the dual stopping rule and the dual duration box are declared in a later dated section (5), before they are coded.

**4.1 Baseline duration repair (candidate A1).** In `GaussianHSMM.fit`, when the guard condition (log-likelihood below the previous one by more than 1e-4, after the convergence test, with previous parameters and the previous E-step statistics available) is met for the first time at an iteration, the engine does not reject: it restores the previous parameters, redoes that M-step with the SAFEGUARDED duration update, and recomputes the E-step at the new parameters (same iteration index). The safeguarded update, per regime with entry weights `w`: candidates (i) the previous iterate, (ii) the old optimiser result (L-BFGS-B from the moment estimate, maxiter 5, ftol 1e-2, with its moment fallback), (iii) L-BFGS-B from the previous iterate with maxiter 200, ftol 1e-12, (iv) the same from the moment estimate; all with sigma in [1e-3, 5] as before; the candidate with the highest `sum(w * log pmf)` wins, ties in the order (iii), (iv), (ii), (i). So the duration block's Q cannot fall. All other blocks are the old M-step on the same statistics. From the first repair on, the safeguarded update is used for every later M-step of that fit ("sticky"). If the repaired parameters still give a drop above 1e-4, the fit ends `REJECTED_UPDATE` with the previous parameters restored, as before. The dropped log-likelihood is not appended to `log_likelihood_history_` (so the history stays monotone) and is recorded in `guard_repairs_` (list of dicts: iteration, drop). A fit that never meets the guard condition runs the old code unchanged: bit-identical by construction and by the identity gate (4.6).
**4.2 Guard tolerance.** Unchanged: baseline 1e-4 absolute (trigger of the repair and of the final rejection), dual 1e-3 (no dual event was observed in 44 fits). Reason: 0 of 138 observed decreases were at noise level, so a looser tolerance would only hide real decreases; after the repair the update is a proper M-step, so a remaining drop above 1e-4 is a real failure and is reported as one.
**4.3 Collapse.** `COLLAPSE_MIN_EFFECTIVE_DAYS = 4.0` in `config.py`: a fit is *collapsed* iff some regime's effective days (sum over rows of its smoothed posterior) are below 4. A variance at the floor (`var <= 1.001e-3`) is REPORTED (`n_variance_at_floor`) but does not make a fit collapsed: in Q2b, floor-only converged fits are not worse than healthy ones (ARI 0.605 against 0.574 in F3). `fit_with_random_restarts` among `CONVERGED` restarts selects the best log-likelihood among the non-collapsed ones (first maximum on ties); if every converged restart is collapsed it returns the best one and marks it `collapsed`; `HSSMResult.withheld_reason` then names `collapsed_regime`. This is a safety rule: Q2b shows no gain in label accuracy from it (paired -0.0015, se 0.0087), and the document says so; the Item 4 table reports its effect.
**4.4 Multi-start metadata (ledger N1).** Additive only. Descriptive only; the Item 4 table gives their rank correlation with the true ARI; no threshold on them changes publishing.

**5.2 Dual duration box (candidate D1), event-triggered.** Box: `mu in [-5, ln(10 * Dmax)]`, `sigma in (0, 3]` (the limits of the declared degeneracy definition of section 3, Q3, and the lower end of the region used there for the nearest ordinary pmf). `_update_discrete_params` runs the Nelder-Mead update exactly as before; only if its result is outside the box (or non-finite) is the update redone from the previous iterate with a penalty that returns 1e12 outside the box. So a dual fit whose Nelder-Mead results never leave the box is bit-identical. Flags: `duration_box_applied_` (list of dicts: iteration, regime) and `duration_at_bound_` (per regime: the final value is within 1e-6 of a bound); the result carries `duration_at_bound` and the count of box applications in `convergence_metadata["duration_box"]` (additive). Nothing is withheld because of the box. Reason: 10 of 40 regimes in the diagnosis users had a degenerate duration (sigma at the wall 10 with mu from -165 to 146, or mu about 10); 7 of 10 are not reparametrisations of an ordinary pmf (KL 0.0013 to 0.0061), so they are fitted artefacts of a flat objective, and the box keeps the optimum in the region where the pmf has a meaning on the lattice. Cost: a regime whose best unconstrained pmf needs sigma above 3 loses a little log-likelihood (reported, flagged `duration_at_bound`).

**5.5 Clarifications written before any dual engine code or dual test exists (dated 2026-11-04; they refine, they do not move a threshold).** (a) *Which results are redone.* The box redo replaces only a Nelder-Mead result that the old rule would have ACCEPTED (optimiser success, finite, sigma above 0) and that lies outside the box. A result the old rule rejected (no success, non-finite, exception) keeps the old path (previous iterate kept, `duration_optimizer_success_[k] = False`, `duration_fallback_used_ = True`), so a fit that ended `CONVERGED` through such a fallback stays bit-identical; the "(or non-finite)" of 5.2 therefore adds nothing beyond the old fallback. If the redo itself does not succeed, the old fallback path is taken. (b) *`duration_at_bound_` tolerance.* 5.2 says 1e-6. Nelder-Mead's default `xatol` and `fatol` are 1e-4, so an optimum that lies on the wall is returned within about 1e-4 of it and a 1e-6 test would never fire; the flag uses an absolute tolerance of `1e-3` in mu and in sigma (new constant `DUAL_DURATION_BOUND_TOL`). Nothing dual has been run under this change yet; the identity gate and the study use this value. (c) *Window and rows.* `T` is `len(X)` (rows, including rows with missing features); the window needs `len(history) > W`, otherwise the fit stays `MAX_ITER_REACHED`. (d) *`n_iter_`.* At the iteration limit `n_iter_` is the number of E-steps run (`len(log_likelihood_history_)`), for both outcomes.
**5.6 Design change to D1 (dated 2026-11-04), written BEFORE the engine is changed, after a development look that I report here.** What I saw: `wp6_evidence/box_firing_dev_design1_user5101.jsonl` (user 5101, one fit, development only): with the box event-triggered at ANY iteration as written in 5.2, the box fired at iteration 0 (regime 1: the Nelder-Mead result went to mu 2.05 -> far above the box and was redone to mu 5.52, the upper wall), and that fit then ended with ordinary durations (mu 2.38 and 2.96, sigma 0.13 and 0.18), the same place as the reference fit up to 1e-6 in the parameters and 3e-3 nats in the log-likelihood. So the design of 5.2 changes a fit that ends non-degenerate, because a transient out-of-box step early in the EM is common; that breaks the owner rule that a fit that was not degenerate must stay bit-identical, and 5.2's sentence "a dual fit whose Nelder-Mead results never leave the box is bit-identical" is true but too weak for that rule. New design (replaces 5.2's trigger, keeps its box, flags and reason): `fit()` first runs the fit EXACTLY as before (box off). Only if that fit ENDS with a regime duration outside the box (the degeneracy of section 3, Q3) it is run once more from the same starting point (the random generator state saved before the first run is restored) with the box active (the event-triggered redo of 5.2 inside the EM loop, penalty outside the box, start clipped); the second fit replaces the first. A fit that ends inside the box is therefore bit-identical to the reference by construction (the box code is never entered), and every changed fit is one whose reference duration was degenerate. Records: `duration_box_refit_` (None, or a dict with the first fit's final state, iterations, log-likelihood and durations), `duration_box_applied_` (of the final fit), `convergence_metadata["duration_box"]` gains `refit`. Switch: class attribute `DualTimescaleHSSM.DURATION_BOX_REFIT = True`. Cost: a fit that ends degenerate is run twice (reported in item 4 as the share of fits refitted). Numerical-health bookkeeping (`fit_cholesky_fallbacks_`, `fit_degraded_`, `fit_exceptions_`) describes the FINAL fit only; the cumulative counter `cholesky_fallback_count_` still counts both runs. Gate (replaces the last clause of 5.4): in the identity batteries and in the item 4 dual arm, among dual fits whose reference final durations are inside the box the number of differing fits must be 0; fits whose reference durations are outside the box are reported separately (count and what changed). Tests and mutants of 5.4 are adapted accordingly (box active flag, refit trigger, generator state restored, refit result kept, first-fit record). Nothing of the stopping rule (5.1) changes.

## 7. Part B: N17, parameter uncertainty in `m_t_std`. Dated 2026-11-15, BEFORE any Part B engine code, study script result or test; seeds 7301..7312 (development) and 7401..7424 (evaluation) are untouched

What I have seen: the WP5 diagnosis (coverage of +-2 `m_t_std`, 24 seeds: personal 0.675 / 0.677, true parameters 0.951 / 0.950; the reading rules point to missing parameter uncertainty only), and sizing numbers from one smoke fit (seed 9001, a seed that is in no study): the forward pass takes 0.06 s, `estimate_states` 0.08 s, the continuous parameter vector has 50 entries, a numeric Hessian of the log-likelihood takes 88 s, and the gauge-projected information matrix of that fit has two negative and a few near-zero eigenvalues (the fit is not at an exact maximum after 20 iterations). Nothing about coverage with a parameter term.

**7.1 The term (derived, not tuned).** Delta method on the smoothed latent mean. Parameters theta = the continuous parameters in unconstrained form (A_k, b_k, log-Cholesky of Q_k, C_k, d_k, log R_k; K = 2, L = 2, F = 4 gives 50). I = minus the Hessian of the engine's own log-likelihood `_forward_pass(X).log_likelihood`, central differences with step 1e-3 (off-diagonal one-sided second differences from the same function). J = d m_t / d theta, central differences of the engine's own `estimate_states` mean, step 1e-4. The latent frame is not identified (m' = S m + c changes A, b, Q, C, d and nothing the model says about X): the six frame directions (L*L + L) are computed from the exact parameter map and projected out, Z = orthonormal basis of their orthogonal complement, Sigma = Z (Z' I Z)^+ Z'. Total variance = `m_t_std`^2 + diag(J Sigma J'). `m_t` is not touched. The discrete parameters (pi, A_slow, durations) are not part of theta (stated limitation: their uncertainty is not propagated).

**7.2 Policy for an information matrix that is not positive definite (the one design choice that development may settle).** Eigenvalues of Z' I Z with absolute value below tol = 4000 * eps_machine * max(|ll|, 1) / h^2 (about 2e-4, the rounding resolution of the difference scheme) are treated as not identified at the data scale and left out of the pseudo-inverse (their number is reported). Remaining negative eigenvalues: alternative **R** (reflect: use |lambda|) or alternative **C** (clip: drop them like the unidentified ones). Rule: whichever of R and C has the mean +-2 coverage nearer to 0.9545 on the development seeds is frozen (commit) before the evaluation seeds are touched; tie or both outside the band of 7.3: R; if both fail, the failure is reported and nothing else is tuned. No other constant is adjustable.

**7.3 Data and arms (the N17 generator: `generate_synthetic_dual_timescale_data(n_regimes=2, n_features=4, latent_dim=2, n_segments=4, mean_dwell=15.0, seed)`, fit as in cell B: `DualTimescaleHSSM(2, 4, 2, max_duration=25, seed=seed).fit(X, n_iter=20)`).** Development 7301..7312, evaluation 7401..7424, once. ### 7.5 Amendment, dated 2026-11-15, written after ONE development seed (7301) and before any other development seed, any evaluation seed or any engine code
What I saw: on seed 7301 (E0 coverage +-2: 0.775 / 0.761; T0 0.958 / 0.958), the term of 7.1 as written makes `m_t_std` about 5.4 times wider on average (alternative R; 4.6 for C) and coverage 1.0 / 1.0 at +-2, in E1 and also in T1 at the true parameters (1.0 / 1.0). Cause (checked on that fit only, by projecting): the model class gives every regime its own C_k, d_k, A_k, b_k, Q_k and the latent is shared only through the few switches, so each regime has its own nearly unidentified latent frame (the test docstring of `tests/test_dual_model_latent_calibration.py` says the latent "is identifiable only up to a map that can differ per regime"); the Hessian is nearly flat along those directions and J is large along them, so the delta method counts as uncertainty what is a change of per-regime frame. Removing only the single global frame in output space (4.1 times) did not help enough; removing the per-regime frame in output space gave ratio 2.7 and coverage 0.972 / 1.0 at +-2, 0.746 / 0.789 at +-1 on that seed (a single seed, numbers not a result).
Design change (structure, not a constant): after J is computed, the change of the smoothed mean caused by a parameter change is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the per-regime frame changes in OUTPUT space: for every regime label r present in the MAP regime sequence `p_t` of the same `estimate_states` call (so the labels are known to the product), the 6 directions "rows of regime r: delta m[:, a] = m[:, b] (a, b in 0..L-1) and delta m[:, a] = 1" (L*L + L per regime). Var_param(m_t) = diag(P_perp J Sigma J' P_perp'). Everything else of 7.1 and 7.2 stands. Stated meaning, to be written into the docs: `m_t_std` is the uncertainty of the latent trajectory modulo a per-regime affine change of its coordinates (the identifiability of the model class); it is not the uncertainty of an absolute latent coordinate. A regime label with so few rows that the 6 directions fill them removes the term on those rows (limitation, reported). The metric of 7.3 (per-true-regime in-sample alignment) is the same kind of object, so the study cannot tell E1 from a version that is too wide in the frame the alignment removes; B2 (T1, E1 against T1) is the guard against that and a ceiling failure is reported as a failure.
Arms on the development seeds additionally report the ablation without the output projection (the 7.1 version) so its size is on record. Criteria B1 to B6 and the evaluation seeds are unchanged.
