## What the numbers say, and what they do not (not hidden)
- **Safety held, coverage did not.** On 216 accepted baseline alignments nothing was wrong and unflagged and every served one was correct (also on both drift protocols and for all three cadences), but the system serves a third of them. The 80% coverage target of the owner's intent is **not reached**; the development shortfall (37.3%) was reported at the freeze, not loosened, and the evaluation confirms it (33.3%). The interval for 1a/1b crosses the thresholds with 72 and 216 observations, so "met" means point estimate only.
- **Why coverage is low is a property of the fits, not of the matcher** (RUN): of the 144 baseline alignments that were flagged, 138 would have been right and 6 wrong when checked against the planted truth (both fits recovering the planted regimes), so most refusals are cautious rather than forced by errors; the identifiability gate is where the safety comes from (in the development study a gate of 3.0 served 63.3% but let 3 strict-wrong alignments through, `tests/WP8_ITEM4_RESULTS.md`). Which gate to accept is an open owner/expert decision.
- **Criterion 3 (same window, different fit seed) is NOT MET: 166/240.** A post-hoc split written **after** the result was seen (`integration_report/wp8/scripts/item8_posthoc_crit3.py`, `wp8_evidence/item8_posthoc_crit3.txt`; it changes no verdict) shows: the two seeds never differ in state (0 cases), there is no case where both were ALIGNED with different stable ids, and all 74 non-identical cases are the same state with stable ids that disagree on more than 1% of days (65 CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED, 9 NEW_LINEAGE). So the differences come from the fits themselves (a different seed gives a different day-level partition of the same window), not from the identity decision; but the criterion as declared is not met and I do not reinterpret it. Nothing in WP8 can make two different fits give the same partition.
- **Dual engine and engine switch: nothing was served and nothing is demonstrated.** Only 13 of 120 dual fits were accepted by the publish rule (17 CONVERGED, 91 MAX_ITER_REACHED, 12 REJECTED_UPDATE); the declared minimum of 20 accepted alignments was not reached for the dual criteria (5, 0 and 1) or the engine switch (14). Their thresholds are uncalibrated by the freeze, so every dual or cross-engine alignment is CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED with reason `threshold_not_calibrated`. This is a limit of what the data can show, not a pass.
- **Criterion 6:** the K_CHANGED flag is reliable (38/38) but the split/merge candidates are not (precision 7.9%, expected >= 80%), and the K selection sees the planted change in only 3/24 streams. The candidates are exploratory by declaration and are **not** ready to show a user.
- **Control 9a is inert:** the z-space control (distance in each fit's own z-space, no stored scaler) changes nothing on these growing windows, so this data does **not** show that the stored scaler is needed. It is reported as inert, not as passed.
- Ladder: 7 of 24 first fits could not be ordered reliably (unverified: the old names are then kept and the message says so).
- Seeds: the evaluation ranges 8301..8324, 8401..8424, 8501..8512, 8601..8612 are now used. They must not be reused for design decisions.

## Post-hoc measurements (written after the results were seen; no verdict and no frozen setting changed)
1. **Criterion 3 split** (`wp8_evidence/item8_posthoc_crit3.txt`): see above.
2. **Margin and gate on held-out data** (`item4_analyze.py` and `item4_margin_report.py` run on the evaluation baseline fits B-G, files `wp8_evidence/item8_posthoc_*`):
   - Margins: of the 216 baseline alignments, **0 permutation errors** (207 had both fits recovering the planted regimes; the 9 others are not-recoverable fits). The 9 strict-wrong alignments (a fit that does not recover the planted regimes) have margin ratios from 0.0499 up, so the margin does not separate them; **the identifiability gate is what carries the safety**, as in development (where permutation errors among recoverable fits began only at margin ratio about 0.48). Of the 72 alignments passing the frozen gate (h = 4.0) all are correct and every one has margin ratio below 0.03.
   - **Gate sensitivity on held-out data (important for the owner's decision on h):** served share and strict-wrong served by gate value on the 216 evaluation alignments: h = 4.0: 72 (33.3%), 0 wrong; h = 3.5: 86 (39.8%), 0; h = 3.0: 118 (54.6%), 0; **h = 2.5: 178 (82.4%), 0**; h = 2.0: 198 (91.7%), 0; h = 0: 209 (96.8%), 2 strict-wrong. In the development study (12 users) h = 3.0 served 63.3% with 3 strict-wrong. The two samples disagree on where the gate should be. **I did not change h** (choosing it on the evaluation data would be selecting on the test set; the declared rule applied to these data would pick 2.5, and 2.5 would meet the 80% coverage criterion on this sample). This is an open decision for the owner or an expert (A7) and for a recalibration on a larger independent set (WP8b).
