## 2. Definitions (used by every item)
- **Accepted fit:** a fit whose `HSSMResult.insights_withheld` is False (the WP6/WP7-R publish rule, not changed here). Lineage is only updated by accepted fits. The share of accepted fits is **reported** in every study; the lineage criteria are computed on accepted fits.
- **Regime summary of a fit:** per regime, the mean observation vector. Baseline: `mu` (in the fit's z-space). Dual: `regime_observation_means(params)` = `d_k + C_k m_k` with the stationary latent mean (frame-free). Raw units: `m_raw = scaler_mean + scaler_std * m_z`, where the scaler is the per-person mean and standard deviation of the fit window that produced the z-scored matrix (stored with the fit; A6).
- **Reference scale:** the `scaler_std` of the lineage's current **anchor** fit. All distances are computed in raw units divided by this one scale, so two fits with different window z-scores are compared in the same units.
- **Distance (Channel 1):** `d(i,j) = sum_f ((m_prev[i,f] - m_new[j,f]) / s_f)^2`, in raw units with the anchor's scale `s_f`. Optional duration term (candidate, Item 2): `lambda * (ln E[dur_i] - ln E[dur_j])^2`.
- **Alignment:** the assignment `perm` (Hungarian) minimising the total cost `C1`. `C2` = the lowest total cost of any other assignment (exhaustive, K <= 6). **Margins stored for every alignment:** `margin_diff = (C2 - C1) / K` (extra cost per regime if the second-best assignment were true) and `margin_ratio = C1 / C2`. Also stored per regime: the distance to the matched regime and to the nearest other regime.
- **Anchor:** the last fit whose state was ALIGNED or NEW_LINEAGE. A fit in CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED or K_CHANGED does **not** move the anchor (fail closed: the next fit is compared with the last confirmed fit).
- **States:** ALIGNED, CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED, K_CHANGED, NEW_LINEAGE (brief). ALIGNED requires: same K as the anchor, `margin_diff >= tau_diff` and `margin_ratio <= rho` (the candidates (a) diff only, (b) ratio only, (c) both are compared in Item 2/development; rule in 4.3), and, if a context channel is supplied, no channel disagreement (section 4.5).
- **Correct alignment (benchmark truth):** every stream has planted regimes with known per-day truth. A fit's regime `j` corresponds to the planted regime with the largest day overlap in that fit's window. The lineage names the planted regimes by the first fit's correspondence. An alignment is **correct** iff every regime of the new fit receives the stable id of the planted regime it corresponds to. A fit whose regimes do not correspond one-to-one to planted regimes (two regimes share the same best planted regime) is **not recoverable**: if it is served as ALIGNED it counts as **wrong**.
- **Served** = state ALIGNED. **Wrong-and-unflagged** = served and not correct. **Flagged** = CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED or K_CHANGED.

## 3. Generators (all synthetic; the sizes are ASSUMPTIONS, there are no real population data here)
Implemented in `data/synthetic_identity.py` after this commit and **before** any scored run; any deviation from this text is a dated change below.
- **Features (F = 7, registry name / unit / source / role / activity direction):** `steps` steps/day movement/movement +1; `time_moving` min/day movement/time_moving +1; `mean_hr` bpm heart_rate/heart_rate +1; `hrv` ms heart_rate/heart_rate -1; `talking` min/day voice/talking +1; `pitch` Hz voice/other 0 (carries regime information, no activity direction); `sleep` h other/other -1. The direction is a domain prior, **not** learned from data or outcomes.
- **Regimes:** K planted (3 unless a study says otherwise), activity levels `alpha_k = linspace(-1, 1, K) * sep`, so the planted order is the index order. Regime mean of feature f: `b_f + s_f * (dir_f * a_f * alpha_k + o_kf)` with loadings `a = (1.0, 0.9, 0.6, 0.5, 0.3, 0, 0.2)`, and regime-specific offsets `o_kf ~ N(0, 0.3^2)` (differences that are **not** along the activity order, so regimes differ in other directions too). `b_f`, `s_f` are physical (steps 7000 +- 2500, minutes 70 +- 25, bpm 72 +- 6, ms 45 +- 12, minutes 90 +- 35, Hz 180 +- 20, hours 7 +- 1).
- **Noise:** day-to-day noise `s_f * sigma_f * e_t` with `sigma = (0.35, 0.45, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 0.8)` and AR(1) persistence 0.4 (a fast component, so the dual model has something to model).
- **Per-user variation (seeded, separate generator stream as in WP5):** separation `sep` log-uniform in [0.5, 2.0]; each loading, each noise level multiplied by `exp(0.25 z)`; per-feature coverage `c = (0.98, 0.97, 0.90, 0.85, 0.75, 0.75, 0.90)` perturbed by +- 0.05; base level shift `s_f * N(0, 0.5^2)`. So the reliability of each feature differs by user.
- **Dwell and switching:** dwell `lognormal(ln 12, 0.35)` clipped to 5..30 days; next regime uniform among the others.
- **Missingness:** per-feature day dropout with probability `1 - c_f`; whole-day missing probability 0.03 (NaN row). The model input is the per-person z-scored window; missing single features are filled with 0 (the person's mean), the declared stand-in for "handled upstream".
- **Window protocol G (growing):** first fit on days [0, 90), then weekly refits on [0, 97), ..., [0, 150). **Protocol R (rolling):** trailing 60-day windows refit weekly, t = 60, 67, ..., 150.
- **Drift:** every regime mean moves linearly over the 150 days by a displacement vector whose Euclidean norm is `drift_sd` in units of the user's within-regime standard deviation (per feature, across the 7 features), along a random unit direction (half common to all regimes, half regime-specific); `drift_sd` is 1 or 2.5. (The brief says "1 and 2.5 sd" without a definition; this is mine.)
- **K change:** a stream with K = 3 for days [0, 90) then (a) a **split**: from day 90 on, a random half of the later segments of one parent regime become a new regime whose mean is the parent's plus 2 within-regime sd along a random direction, or (b) a **merge**: from day 90 on, one regime's mean equals another's (abrupt). Truth gives the parent regime(s).
- **Engine switch:** the same window fitted with the baseline and with the dual engine.
- **Fit settings:** `fit_hssm`, `candidate_ks = (K_planted,)` for criteria 1 to 5, 7 and 8 (isolates identity from K selection, which WP7-R studied); `(2, 3, 4)` for criterion 6. Baseline: `n_initializations = 10`, `max_iter = 60`. Dual: `n_initializations = 2` with `allow_fast_test_fit`, `max_iter = 60` unless development shows too few accepted dual fits, in which case `max_iter` is raised (the compute cost is the reason); frozen in the design freeze.

## 4. Design and the rules for numbers

### 4.2 Ordering ladder (Item 3)
- **Score:** `score_k = sum_f w_f * dir_f * (m_raw[k,f] - mean_f) / s_f` over the rung's features. `dir_f` from the registry (prior, not data).
- **Weights from reliability, not from outcome labels or evaluation seeds:** `w_f proportional to c_f * SNR_f / (1 + SNR_f)` with `SNR_f` = between-regime variance / within-regime variance of feature f over the decoded regimes of the fit window, `c_f` the observed share of days; weights normalised to sum 1; features with `dir_f = 0` get weight 0. The function takes no label argument (test).
- **Rungs:** 1 = all features with `dir_f != 0`; 2 = `steps` and `time_moving` (role movement, time_moving); 3 = `steps` only.
- **Reliability test (declared now):** `B = 200` block-bootstrap resamples of the window's days (blocks of 7 days), recomputing the regime feature means from the **same decoded labels** (no refit), then the score. A rung is **reliable** iff (i) the modal ordering of the regimes has frequency >= **0.90** and (ii) the smallest adjacent score gap of the point estimate is >= `g_min`. `g_min` is **fixed by rule**: the smallest value on development seeds above which the modal-order accuracy against the planted order is 100%, rounded up to one decimal; if no such value exists below 1.0, I report that and use 0.5.
- **Agreement check (my addition to the brief's design, reason: a rung can be stable and still wrong when a misleading feature has high SNR):** if a higher rung is reliable but a lower rung that is itself reliable gives a different order, the ordering is **unverified** (conflict), not served as verified.
- **Choice:** the highest reliable rung with no conflict; else **unverified**: ids follow first-fit order (the order of the fit's raw regime indices), no activity claim (`activity_rung = 0`, `ordering_verified = False`). Recorded: `activity_rung`, weights, margins, verified flag. The ordering is applied **only at the first accepted fit** of a lineage; later fits inherit names by alignment (never re-sorted).
- **Criterion L (new, my addition; ladder claims must be true):** among fits whose ordering is declared verified, **>= 90%** match the planted order (K = 3, chance 1/6 = 16.7%); the unverified share and the per-rung counts are reported. Reason for 90%: the 0.90 bootstrap threshold implies about that much correctness if calibrated; the study tests whether it is. Evaluated on the first fits (window 90) of 8301..8324.
### 4.3 Alignment threshold rule (Item 4, development seeds 8201..8212)
Candidates: distance {z-space without the stored scaler, raw, raw + duration with `lambda` in {0.5, 1}} (z-space is a negative control, not a candidate) times acceptance {(a) `margin_diff >= tau`, (b) `margin_ratio <= rho`, (c) both}. On the development alignments (protocols G and R, drift 0, 1 and 2.5, both engines, plus engine-switch pairs; at least 200 alignments per engine) choose, per acceptance, the **smallest** `tau` (or **largest** `rho`) with **0 wrong-and-unflagged** development alignments, report the coverage it gives; choose the candidate with the highest coverage; ties go to the simpler one (raw, then (b), then (a), then (c)). The chosen numbers go into the Design freeze. If no candidate reaches **80%** coverage with 0 wrong-served on development, I say so and the owner sees the shortfall; I do not loosen the criteria.
### 4.4 Lineage, A3 data model (Item 4)
Pure functions in `backbone/hssm/lineage.py` (new module, additive), result fields `stable_regime_id`, `lineage_id`, `lineage_epoch`, `continuity_state`, `alignment_margin` (= `margin_diff`; `margin_ratio` and per-regime distances alongside), `activity_rung`, `scaler_mean`, `scaler_std`, `expert_review_status`, plus `proposed_new_names`, `user_choice` in {None, KEEP_OLD, NEW_NAMES}, `message` ("cannot confirm this is the same as before"), `split_merge_candidates`. `None` behaves as `KEEP_OLD`. `NEW_NAMES` starts a new epoch and records the old-to-new mapping permanently. No consumer may say "same as before" unless `continuity_state == ALIGNED` (a helper `may_claim_same_as_before(record)`). Baseline and dual both go through the same summary interface; dual regime-indexed outputs are permuted with `permute_regimes`. The model objects and the raw `HSSMResult` numeric outputs are **not** mutated (A4), so previously healthy fits stay bit-identical. `canonicalize_labels`: docstring and the constant-name claim fixed (the convention is **not** an activity ordering; the constant value keeps working), no behaviour change (test with `ast`-level and numeric checks).
### 4.5 Two channels (Item 5)
Channel 1 as above with a feature registry (name, unit, source, role, direction). Channel 2 (optional): per-regime context profile (non-negative vector summing to 1, supplied by the caller), distance = squared Hellinger. Combined cost `w1 * d1 / scale1 + w2 * d2 / scale2` with configurable weights (default `w2 = 0`). **Disagreement rule:** if each channel alone gives a confident alignment (its own margin above its own threshold) but the two assignments differ, the state is CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED. Tests with synthetic descriptors only. The contract doc states that contextual descriptors are sensitive and must come from a consented, aggregated, non-identifying pipeline. No topic or scene extraction is built.
### 4.6 Refit policy (Item 6)
`RefitPolicy(refit_interval_days = 7)`, bounds **1 <= interval <= 28** (reason: below one day there is no new data per refit; above four weeks the stable ids of a user can no longer be tracked through drift of 1 sd per 150 days in the study, and the gap to the last anchor grows); `should_refit(last_refit_day, today, policy)` pure. Study: the same 150-day planted streams refitted daily, weekly and every 14 days; changing the interval is a config change only (test).
### 4.7 Expert-review tagging (Item 7)
Tag `EXPERT_REVIEW(identity-distance)` at every definition and use of the matching distance, the ladder weights and the alignment thresholds; `docs/EXPERT_REVIEW_REGISTER.md` generated by a script; a test fails if a tagged site is missing from the register or the register lists a site that no longer exists; `expert_review_status = "PENDING"` on every record that used the distance; contract line. It stays PENDING until the owner records a sign-off.

### 2026-11-25, Pre-declaration (Item 4): written before any Item 4 development fit was run
Seeds touched so far in Item 4: none (Item 3 used 8201..8212 for the ladder only). The Item 4 development fits (below) are the first fits of this item.
1. **Correctness of an alignment (refines section 2; the strict reading stays the criterion).** Anchor fit A, new fit N, `perm[i]` = the regime of N matched to regime i of A. A fit is *recoverable* as in section 2 (its regimes map one-to-one to planted regimes by day overlap). The alignment is **permutation-correct** iff both fits are recoverable and `planted(N, perm[i]) == planted(A, i)` for every i. **Strict (criterion 1 as declared):** correct = permutation-correct, so an ALIGNED alignment that involves a fit that did not recover the planted regimes counts as **wrong**. **Conditional (reported next to it, never in its place):** among alignments whose two fits are both recoverable, the share that is permutation-correct, with the number excluded. Wrong-and-unflagged = strict-wrong and `ALIGNED`.
2. **Fit-identifiability gate (new, from the Item 2 diagnosis: AUC 0.90 / 0.86).** `separation_min(fit)` = the smallest, over regime pairs, Euclidean norm of the difference of the two regime means (raw units) divided feature by feature by the pooled within-regime sd of the days decoded into those regimes (observed entries; a feature with sd 0 gets 1). A pair is `CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED` (reason `fit_not_identifiable`) if the new fit **or the anchor** has `separation_min < h`. **Rule for h** (baseline development alignments, all protocols and drifts, consecutive-refit pairs): grid {0, 0.5, ..., 5.0}; h = the smallest grid value at which **no** alignment whose two fits are both at least h is strict-wrong; report the share of alignments that pass the gate. If no grid value gives 0 strict-wrong, h = the smallest grid value with at most 1% strict-wrong among passing alignments; if none, h = 3.0 (the round value nearest the median separation of recoverable fits in the diagnosis, 3.0 to 3.8) and the shortfall is reported. One h for both engines and for engine switches; if the dual development data say otherwise I report it, I do not silently add a second h.
3. **Margin thresholds** by section 4.3 applied to the alignments that pass the gate, per pair class (baseline to baseline, dual to dual, cross-engine) with its own `tau`/`rho`, with two additions: **`rho` is capped at 0.5** (the production rule `AMBIGUITY_RATIO` of `shrinkage.match_regimes`: the new rule must not be more lenient than the one that exists), and when the rule gives a vacuous threshold (every passing alignment is correct, so the smallest `tau` is 0) the threshold is the **declared floor** `rho = 0.5` and `tau = 0.0` with the reason stated. Coverage = share of **all** development alignments (including those the gate flags) that end `ALIGNED`.
4. **Development streams (seeds 8201..8212, K = 3, fit seed = user seed for every refit).** Protocol G: windows [0, t) for t = 90, 97, ..., 146 and 150 (10 fits); protocol R: trailing 60 days, t = 60, 70, ..., 150 (10 fits; every 10 days instead of weekly for compute, a deviation from section 3 that applies to development only). Baseline: every user at drift 0, 1 and 2.5 (12 x 3 x 20 = 720 fits). Dual (frozen settings, section 8 Item 3): every user at **one** drift (8201..8204: 0; 8205..8208: 1; 8209..8212: 2.5), G and R: 240 fits (a deviation from "all drifts" for compute). Alignments are between consecutive fits of one protocol (18 per stream); engine-switch pairs are the baseline and the dual fit of the same window of the same stream, in both directions. Dual alignments use every dual fit that did not fail numerically, accepted or not (it is geometry for choosing thresholds, not a publish claim).
5. **State machine.** anchor = the last fit whose state was ALIGNED or NEW_LINEAGE; `CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED` and `K_CHANGED` keep the old names, do not move the anchor, and say "cannot confirm this is the same as before"; `K_CHANGED` whenever the fitted K differs from the anchor's K (margins are not consulted); split/merge candidates by nearest assignment in raw units; `NEW_NAMES` starts a new epoch and stores the old-to-new mapping permanently; `None` behaves as `KEEP_OLD`.
6. **Cost (announced to the owner before the run):** baseline fits about 40 minutes wall on 2 cores, dual fits about 2.5 hours.
