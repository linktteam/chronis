Reviewer: 5.4%. Reason, seen in the data: the regime nearest the person's own average has the smallest norm after per-person z-scoring, and for three regimes that is the middle one.
2. **Raw ids move when the window grows (reproduced in direction):** fitted on days 0..90 versus 0..150, raw labels agree on **0.684** of the first 90 days; 9 of 12 users (75%) have more than 5% of days relabelled (8 of 12 at 120 days, agreement 0.700). Reviewer: 0.76 at 120 days, 0.63 at 150, about 30% of users.
3. **But relabelling does not repair it (NOT reproduced):** after the best relabelling the agreement is **0.888** at 150 days, not the reviewer's 0.999. In my generator a longer window also changes where the fit puts its regime boundaries; renaming alone cannot fix that.
4. **Same data, different fit seed (NOT reproduced):** baseline raw agreement **0.951** at 90 days (identical raw labels in 6 of 12 pairs) and 0.981 at 150 days (10 of 12); reviewer: 0.999.
5. **Alignment by regime means made no permutation error where it could be judged (reproduces "23 of 23" in spirit):** of 384 alignments (consecutive growing windows, first-to-later, rolling 60-day windows with drift 1 and 2.5 sd), **298 involve two fits that both recover the planted regimes one-to-one and all 298 are correct**. The other 86 involve a fit whose regimes do not correspond one-to-one to planted regimes (recovery failure); they are counted as wrong by the pre-declared definition. Raw units and z-space are equally correct; raw separates wrong from right better under drift (section 3).
6. **No margin separates the wrong alignments (answer to "the margin below which alignments are wrong"):** there is none. The largest `margin_diff` among wrong alignments is 2.6 (raw), above the median of the correct ones (2.1); the smallest `margin_ratio` among wrong alignments is 0.022. With the existing WP5 rule (`best <= 0.5 * second`), **39 of 335 served alignments (11.6%) are wrong**, i.e. 10.2% of all alignments are wrong and unflagged, against the 1% target. The wrongness comes from fit recovery, not from ambiguity between regimes.
7. **A within-fit signal does discriminate (new):** the smallest pairwise separation between a fit's own regime means, in within-regime standard deviations, is higher for fits that recover the planted regimes (AUC 0.90 growing windows, 0.86 rolling windows). This is a candidate gate for Item 4; it is not yet a criterion.
8. **Dual without canonicalization:** raw agreement 0.71 (90 to 120 days) and 0.74 (90 to 150), **same data different seed 0.228 raw and 0.680 after the best relabelling**, order equals the planted order in 8.3% of fits, only 62.5% of dual fits recover the planted regimes, 0 of 24 are accepted. The dual path here is much less stable than the baseline and its labels are the **filtered** (causal) posterior.
9. **`permute_regimes` covers every regime-indexed dual parameter** (property test, 12 seeds, 32 tests with the baseline ones, 21 of 21 mutants killed); the baseline's six regime-indexed parameters are covered by `canonicalize_labels`. Regime-indexed bookkeeping that neither touches is listed in section 2.
10. **K change:** the matching cannot see it (a plain alignment under the WP5 0.5 ratio rule would be served in 11 of 12 cases); a split points at the true parent regime in 5 of 6 cases, a merge names the true pair in 1 of 6 (exploratory, section 5).

## 2. Regime-indexed parameters of each engine, and the permutation property (RUN + READ)
**Baseline `GaussianHSMM` (READ in `model.py`, confirmed by the test):** `pi` (K), `A` (K x K), `mu` (K x F), `var` (K x F), `dur_mu` (K), `dur_sigma` (K). The test multiplies every array with leading dimension K by a random factor and checks which ones change the log-likelihood: exactly these six. `canonicalize_labels` permutes all six (plus the dicts `duration_optimizer_success_` and `duration_optimizer_messages_`); applying any permutation to them leaves the likelihood unchanged and permutes the posterior columns (12 seeds, tests).
**Dual `DualTimescaleHSSM`:** twelve: `pi`, `A_slow`, `dur_mu`, `dur_sigma`, `A_fast`, `b_fast`, `Q_fast`, `m0`, `P0`, `C_obs`, `d_obs`, `R_obs` = exactly `shrinkage.PARAM_NAMES`; the test finds the same twelve by perturbation (and no other array), and `permute_regimes` changes every one of them for a generic model. Property (seeds 8701..8712, one random model and one random permutation each): the log-likelihood is unchanged, the filtered posterior columns are permuted (`new[:, k] = old[:, perm[k]]`), the decoded label is renamed by the inverse permutation, the smoothed latent trajectory is unchanged. Negative control: skipping any single parameter in the permutation is detected for all twelve. **32 tests; mutants 21 of 21 killed** (`wp8_evidence/mutation_item2.txt`).
**Regime-indexed bookkeeping that is NOT a parameter and is NOT permuted by either function (READ; it keeps raw indices):** dual `duration_optimizer_success_`, `transition_fallback_rows_`, `duration_box_applied_` (a list of dicts with a `regime` field), `duration_box_refit_`; baseline `collapse_report_` (effective days per regime), `restart_summary_`. Item 4 will therefore store these raw-indexed together with the `raw_to_stable` map instead of rewriting them. UNVERIFIED: whether the baseline's `collapse_report_` is computed before or after `canonicalize_labels` (it is not used by anything WP8 builds).

Every alignment is stored with `margin_diff = (C2 - C1)/K`, `margin_ratio = C1/C2`, the per-regime matched distances and nearest-other distances. Two extra candidates are summarised: `max_matched` (largest per-regime distance) and `max_regime_ratio` (largest matched / nearest-other).

Of the 86 pairs that involve a non-recoverable fit, 18 are nevertheless consistent with the best-overlap mapping (the permutation agrees with the planted overlap) and 68 are not.
Margins, all alignments, raw distance: `margin_diff` correct min 0.61, median 2.10; wrong (by the declared definition) max 2.60 (z: 2.58, raw_dur: 2.68). `margin_ratio` correct max 0.612, median 0.055; wrong min 0.022. The ranges overlap completely, so **there is no margin below which alignments are wrong**; the share of all alignments that a threshold placed just beyond every wrong one would still serve is 28% (diff) or 27% (ratio) in raw units (z: 29%, 25%; raw_dur: 27%, 23%).
What the WP5 rule (`best <= 0.5 * second`) would have done (raw): served 335 of 384 (87%); wrong among served 39 (11.6%); **wrong-and-unflagged 10.2% of all** (z: 10.7%, raw_dur: 6.2%). By set: growing consecutive 4.2%, first-to-later 17.5%, drift 1 sd 11.1%, drift 2.5 sd 6.9%. So the reviewer's open question is answered: **the existing 0.5 ratio would NOT have flagged the misses**, because they are not ambiguity between two regimes but fits that did not find the planted regimes.
Raw versus z-space: identical correctness in my data (the windows overlap, so the z-scoring basis hardly moves). Under drift the raw distance separates wrong from right better: smallest wrong `margin_ratio` 0.177 (raw) against 0.065 (z) at 1 sd, 0.151 against 0.095 at 2.5 sd; the share of alignments below the smallest wrong ratio is 42% against 12% (1 sd) and 46% against 31% (2.5 sd). The reviewer's margins (raw 6.2 against z 3.6) are not comparable (different margin definition) but the direction (raw better) is reproduced under drift only. The duration term does not improve the separation (the share of alignments beyond every wrong one is 27% against 28% for `margin_diff`, 23% against 27% for `margin_ratio`, 18% against 23% for the other two, raw_dur against raw); under the fixed 0.5 ratio rule it serves fewer alignments (83% against 87%) and fewer wrong ones (6.2% against 10.2% of all), and the two effects cannot be separated here, so development has to decide.
**Fit-level signal (exploratory, section 7 of the analysis):** min pairwise regime separation in within-regime sd, baseline fits that recover the planted regimes: median 3.01 [1.99..6.52], not recovering: 2.31 [1.62..2.76]; AUC 0.90 (growing windows, 6 non-recoverable); rolling windows 3.79 [1.95..7.02] against 2.67 [1.06..3.53], AUC 0.86 (42 non-recoverable). The smallest decoded regime size is weaker (AUC 0.90 and 0.64). This is the candidate for a fit-identifiability gate.

## 5. K change (baseline, K = 3 on days 0..90, K = 4 or 2 on days 30..150; RUN, exploratory)
If K were NOT changed (K = 3 forced on days 30..150), a plain alignment to the first fit would be **served in 11 of 12 cases** under the WP5 rule (ratio <= 0.5; 6 of 6 for the split streams, 5 of 6 for the merge streams; margin ratio median 0.21 and 0.26) and is correct in 5/6 (split) and 3/6 (merge): the matching cannot tell that the world changed; the K selection must supply that signal (`K_CHANGED` is decided from K, not from margins).
With K changed: for a **split** (3 to 4 regimes) the old regime that is nearest to two new regimes is the true parent in **5 of 6** users (the sixth: the first fit did not recover the parent); for a **merge** (3 to 2) the two old regimes nearest to the same new regime are the true merged pair in **1 of 6** (the window 30..150 mixes 60 days before and 60 after the change, so the K = 2 regime is a blend). Split/merge candidates will be reported as candidates with this precision, never as continuity.

## 8. What this means for the design (consequences, to be tested in Items 3 and 4 on development seeds)
1. The name ordering must not use the L2 convention (7.7%); Item 4 fixes its docstring and constant-name claim without changing behaviour.
2. The alignment by raw regime means is not where the risk is (0 permutation errors among recoverable fits). **The risk is serving a claim of continuity for a fit that did not find the regimes.** The pre-declared strict criterion 1 counts such a served alignment as wrong, which makes it a test of fit quality as much as of alignment. I will (a) add a fit-identifiability gate to the lineage states (candidate: min pairwise regime separation below a threshold set on development data gives `CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED`) and (b) report criterion 1 **both** as declared (strict) and conditional on recoverable fits, in a dated section before Item 8 is run. The strict reading will probably fail on rolling 60-day windows whatever the lineage does; that is a finding, not something to hide.
3. Keep raw units (owner decision A6; also better under drift here); z-space stays as the negative control. The duration term did not improve the separation of wrong from right; it stays a candidate only through the declared development rule (section 4.3 of the pre-declaration).
4. `K_CHANGED` must come from the K comparison, not from margins. Split candidates are usable (5/6), merge candidates are weak (1/6); both are reported as candidates only.
5. Dual: 0 of 24 fits accepted at `max_iter` 60 and only 62.5% recover the regimes; the dual lineage criteria will probably be UNKNOWN under the declared rule (fewer than 20 accepted alignments) unless development finds settings that give accepted dual fits. I will test `max_iter` 200 on development seeds and report what it costs.
6. Ladder weights should follow reliability (talking and sleep are near chance; steps, mean_hr, time_moving are informative); the agreement check between rungs is kept.
