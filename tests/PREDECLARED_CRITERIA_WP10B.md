## 4. C1(2): a mildly wrong prior (design frozen by this commit)

### 4.1 Question
Does `certify_prior_anchored` refuse a prior that is harmful but milder than H3 (every intercept + 16)? WP9 and WP10A found that +1.5, +4, +8 and a scrambled prior did not pass the declared harmful-prior rule. WP10A rule (a) (the control fit must be worse than the personal fit, no prior, by 0.25 nats/step) failed for H1 and H2 only because the personal fits at 30 and 60 days are catastrophic, so any prior beats them. For a control meant to test a certificate that compares against the **single Gaussian**, the relevant property is "worse than the single Gaussian", which is rule (b) and (c) of WP10A. This part therefore uses (b) and (c) unchanged and **drops (a)**; that change is stated here, before any fit, and is not a silent move of a target. The WP10A result for H3 stands as it is.

### 4.2 Setting (as WP10A A6 part 2: `a6_harm_run.py`)
K = 2, F = 4, L = 2, lengths 30 / 60 / 120 days (2 / 4 / 8 segments), 200 iterations, 1 restart, fast-test switch and cold-start gate off for the study only, n0 = 5, public path `fit_hssm(..., certify_prior_anchored=True)`, Dmax 25, population `PopulationSpec()` users, true prior `wp7r_evidence/prior_main_wp5.json`. The fit seed is `seed - 2000` as in A6. The runner is `integration_report/wp10b/scripts/c1_mild_run.py` (a copy of the A6 runner with the candidate construction below; unchanged scoring).

### 4.3 Candidates (all built from the true prior; all milder than H3 BY CONSTRUCTION, defined as: the Frobenius norm of the change of `d_obs` is strictly smaller than H3's, 16 * sqrt(8) = 45.25)
- **M1:** every intercept + 8 (the WP10A H2; norm 22.6). Re-run on fresh seeds because WP10A saw it only on the WP9 development users.
- **M2:** every intercept + 12 (norm 33.9).
- **M3:** regime-swapped observation model: `d_obs`, `C_obs`, `R_obs` of regime 0 and regime 1 exchanged, every other parameter (dynamics, durations, initial state) kept (norm of the `d_obs` change = the distance between the two regimes' intercept vectors times sqrt(2), a few units at most; the exact value is printed by the runner).
- Mechanisms not tried because they cannot be built in this setting without a new generator: "a prior from a different generator family" and "users with a very different regime count" (the shared prior has a fixed K = 2 and F = 4 here). Said now so it is not read as a result.

### 4.4 Development (seeds 11001..11012, 3 lengths = 36 fits per candidate; each candidate is fitted on the same users and lengths; the single-Gaussian score of each user and length is the production `n18v2.gauss_scores`)
- **Validity (rule applied to each candidate):** (b) at least 6 of the 36 fits have a held-out one-step mean log-score below the same user and length's single Gaussian; (c) at least 4 of those are eligible for the certificate (dual fit, EM state REJECTED_UPDATE or MAX_ITER_REACHED, no other withhold reason: the A6 definition). A candidate that meets both is VERIFIED HARMFUL (mild).
- **Frozen candidate:** among the verified candidates the one with the smallest norm of the `d_obs` change (the mildest, so the hardest test of the certificate). If none is verified: **no mild harmful control could be built, the evaluation is NOT run**, the report says so, and C1(2) ends as "no mild control could be made harmful in this setting; the +16 control remains the only valid one" (this is a legitimate outcome and not a failure of the package).
- The freeze is written to `tests/DESIGN_FREEZE_WP10B_C1.txt` (first line `{"candidate": "M1"|"M2"|"M3"}`) and committed before the evaluation starts; the runner refuses the evaluation without it.

### 4.5 Evaluation (seeds 11021..11060 x 3 lengths = 120 fits per arm; arms M (the frozen candidate) and T (the true prior); the single Gaussian per user and length)
- **Eligible harmful fit:** an arm-M fit that is eligible for the certificate AND whose held-out one-step mean log-score is below the single Gaussian's.
- **Required number: at least 12 eligible harmful fits** (as WP10A; 0 certified among 12 gives an exact one-sided 95% upper bound of 22%). Decided blind to the certification outcome: the count is computed by a function that never reads the certification field. If the 40 users give fewer than 12, the **pre-declared extension** adds seeds 11061..11080 once; if still fewer than 12 the verdict is **NOT EVALUABLE** (control insufficient) and no claim is made.
- **E1 (primary):** among eligible harmful fits, the number certified (`CERTIFIED_PRIOR_ANCHORED`). **MET** if 0 are certified with n >= 12 (report the exact one-sided 95% bound); **NOT MET** if at least 1 is certified (report k of n and which lengths); NOT EVALUABLE if n < 12 after the extension. The result is "the certificate refuses mild harm in this setting", never "refuses any harmful prior".
- **E0 (informative, reported):** the share of eligible arm-T fits that are certified (WP10A: 13 of 25). If it is 0 the test of refusal is vacuous and this is said.
- **E3 (reported):** how many arm-M fits that are worse than the single Gaussian are published after the certificate (withhold reason None). Expected 0; any nonzero is reported as a finding.
- Failure conditions: any crash in a fit is recorded as "no publishable output" (as A6), never dropped; a non-finite score counts as harmful and not certified.

## 5. C2: the hard-stratum ceiling (design frozen by this commit)

### 5.1 Question
28 of 36 hard users never received a continuity claim in WP10A. Is that because the data cannot support one (withholding is correct), or because the fit is not good enough (better fitting could serve them)? Measured as: what would the lineage gate say if the matcher were given the TRUE regime assignment?

### 5.2 Data and quantities
- Users and windows as WP10A A3 seed-A chains: baseline generator `generate_identity_stream` per stratum, windows [0, t) with t = 90, 97, ..., 146, 150 (10 per user), K = 3, seven features. **Production fit:** baseline engine, K = 3, 60 EM iterations, 10 restarts, production restart selection (`wp9_fit_lib.fit_restarts`, fit seed = user). Script `integration_report/wp10b/scripts/c2_run.py` (reuses `wp9_fit_lib`; resumable; one jsonl line per fit).
- **Gate quantity.** `lineage.separation_min(raw_means, raw_window, labels)` = the smallest over regime pairs of the Euclidean norm of the mean difference divided feature by feature by the pooled within-regime sd (the sd from the days assigned to the regimes). The lineage gate passes a fit when this is at least h = 2.5 (the product operating point; the consensus flag is a separate condition, reported separately).
- **Production arm P:** `raw_means` and labels from the production-selected restart (as the WP9/WP10A replay: `wp9_replay_lib.wp8_record`), `raw_window` = the window's raw matrix.
- **Oracle arm O (primary):** labels = the TRUE labels of the window's days (`labels_true` of the stored record); `raw_means` = the sample mean of the window's raw rows per true regime; pooled sd from the same true labels. This is the best a perfect labeller with estimated means could achieve on that window. An oracle refit is **evaluable** only if each of the K = 3 true regimes has at least 3 days in the window; non-evaluable windows are counted and reported, and are left out of the primary fraction (a conservative choice against the conclusion "not servable": see the rule below).
- **Reference arm G (secondary):** `raw_means` = the generator's per-day true means (`stream.truth['means_t']`) averaged over the window's days of each true regime, true labels. Reported as the separation in gate units that the generator actually plants; the planted `sep` of a stratum is an assumption, so the realised gate-unit separation is **measured here, not assumed**.
- Metrics, per stratum: (a) the distribution of the oracle `separation_min` per refit (median, quartiles) and of the production one; (b) the fraction of evaluable refits with `separation_min` >= 2.5, for O and for P; (c) the oracle-minus-production gap in that fraction, with a user-level bootstrap interval of the paired difference (resample users; every user contributes all its windows); (d) per user: the share of users whose oracle passes at least once; (e) for P also the fraction passing both the separation gate and not flagged FIT_UNSTABLE (`fit_stability.assess_consensus`, as the replay).

### 5.3 Interpretation rule (declared in advance; applied to the **hard** stratum on the evaluation set; medium and easy are reported with no pass/fail)
Let pO = the fraction of evaluable hard refits whose oracle separation is at least 2.5, pP = the same for the production fit (user-level bootstrap intervals).
- **NOT SERVABLE BY DESIGN** (withholding is correct; hard is documented that way): pO < 0.50. Worded "demonstrated" only if the upper end of the interval of pO is below 0.50, otherwise "on the point estimate".
- **NEEDS BETTER FITTING (WP8b candidate):** pO >= 0.50 AND the gap pO - pP >= 0.20 with the lower end of the paired-difference interval above 0.
- **MIXED / NOT DECIDED:** any other combination; both numbers are reported and no recommendation is made beyond that.
The rule is stated for the hard stratum because the brief asks about it; whether to apply a different level to medium or easy is the owner's decision after reading the table.

## 8. Part 2 (dated 2026-11-27, added after writing the C2 scripts and BEFORE any C2 fit; it closes gaps in section 5, it moves no level)
- **pP in the interpretation rule (5.3) is the production fit's separation-only pass share:** among the oracle-evaluable refits, the share whose production-selected restart is usable (a converged, non-collapsed restart exists) AND has `separation_min` >= 2.5. A refit without a usable production restart counts as not passing. Reason: the rule asks whether the FIT can reach the separation the gate needs; the FIT_UNSTABLE flag (consensus, sharpness below 0.95) is a separate stability control. The share that also requires "not flagged" is reported next to it (`pP_gate`) and is not used by the rule.
- **Evaluable window (5.2), as implemented:** each of the three true regimes has at least 3 days in the window AND at least one observed value for every feature (otherwise no sample mean exists). Non-evaluable windows are counted and reported and left out of every fraction, for the oracle and for the production arm alike (so both arms are measured on the same refits).
- **Intervals (5.2):** every fraction is a ratio over pooled evaluable refits of the users in the resample (sum of passes / sum of evaluable windows), user-level bootstrap with 2000 resamples, percentile 95%, seed 11999; the paired gap pO - pP is computed inside each resample on the same users. The "naive" Wilson interval over refits is reported for the oracle only.
- **Generator-means arm (G):** reported as a reference for what the generator plants in gate units; it is not used by the rule.
- **Development result handling:** the development numbers are reported in full; the interpretation rule is printed for the evaluation only.

### 9.3 What B1 records and how a fit is classified
- Per dual fit: the EM state, every withhold reason, the EM log-likelihood history, the number of iterations, the rule that ended the fit, the gain over the last ten iterations, the duration parameters, whether each regime is at a wall of the duration box or has a pmf still rising at the last lattice step, the minimum effective days of a regime, numerical-health counts, whether the duration-box refit fired, the held-out one-step mean of the dual fit, of the baseline engine and of the single Gaussian.
- **Publishable** = `withheld_reason is None`. **Categories** of a non-publishable fit (a fit can carry several): `iteration_cap` (state MAX_ITER_REACHED), `rejected_update` (REJECTED_UPDATE: the EM monotonicity guard stopped the fit), `other_non_convergence`, `degenerate_duration`, `collapsed_regime`, `numerical` (Cholesky fallback, NUMERICAL_FAILURE, the states pass, late fallbacks), `other`. The exclusive combinations are reported as well.
- **EM creep** (as WP7-R S1): the median ratio of consecutive positive log-likelihood increments over the last 50 iterations and the number of further iterations that rate extrapolates to bring the increment below 1e-4; the share of not-finished fits that finish within +200, +400, +800 and +1600 further iterations is tabulated.
- Descriptive study: **no pass/fail level**. **Failure of the diagnosis itself** (the diagnosis is then repeated before B2, not interpreted): more than 10% of the 36 fits fail to run, or more than 10% of the non-publishable fits carry only `other` (or no category at all).

### 9.4 Declared predictions: which reason each fix is expected to remove (stated before any B1 number; compared with what B2 observes)
- **D60 (Dmax = 60):** removes `degenerate_duration` where the cause is the truncation (a pmf still rising at the last lattice step, or a parameter at the wall because the true duration exceeds Dmax). Expected to remove at least half of the fits that carry it (WP7-R: 8 of 9). Expected NOT to remove `iteration_cap`, `rejected_update` or `collapsed_regime`. It costs run time (a larger lattice).
- **I600 (600 iterations, i.e. +400):** removes `iteration_cap` in the fits that B1's creep extrapolation puts within +400 further iterations; expected NOT to remove `rejected_update` (a stop by the monotonicity guard is not a budget problem), `degenerate_duration` or `collapsed_regime`. The prediction is the B1 count printed on the last line of the analysis.
- **Duration regularisation (REG):** removes `degenerate_duration` caused by a parameter at a wall; expected NOT to remove the others. Its design is declared with B2.
- **Accelerated EM (SQUAREM-S3):** if implemented (decided in B2 after reading B1), expected to remove `iteration_cap` in fits that need up to a few hundred further iterations; expected NOT to remove `degenerate_duration` or `collapsed_regime`; accepted only if held-out prediction does not get worse (levels declared with B2).
- No fix is expected to remove `collapsed_regime` or `numerical`; if those are frequent they are reported as unaddressed.
