## 4. A1 diagnosis design (read-only; this part is committed before it runs)
Question (brief A1.1): on seeds 9201..9212, how often do two seeds of the same data give different partitions, as a function of the number of restarts (4, 10, 20), the selection rule (best log-likelihood among CONVERGED non-collapsed restarts, the production rule), and the distance of the partitions (adjusted Rand index)? - **Data:** users 9201..9204 hard, 9205..9208 medium, 9209..9212 easy; windows [0, 90) and [0, 150); baseline engine, K = 3 fixed, 60 EM iterations, production fit code restart by restart (`integration_report/wp9/scripts/wp9_fit_lib.py`; the selected restart is bit-identical to `fit_hssm` for the same seeds, test `tests/test_wp9_fit_lib.py`). Two fit seeds per window: `user` and `user + 1000`. 20 restarts per fit; the 4- and 10-restart results are the first 4 and the first 10 restarts of the same run (that is exactly what a run with that many restarts does, the seeds are consecutive). 48 fits, about 12 minutes on 2 cores, background, resumable.
- **Measured for each (window, restarts n in 4, 10, 20):** the two selected partitions' ARI; the share of pairs with ARI >= 0.99 ("same partition"), >= 0.9, and below 0.9; the log-likelihood gap between the two selected restarts; whether each selected partition recovers the planted one (ARI to the planted labels >= 0.6 is "recovers"; this is the WP8 convention `recoverable`, kept); the agreement among the best 3 restarts within one fit (minimum pairwise ARI) and the number of distinct partitions (ARI < 0.9) among converged non-collapsed restarts.
- **Classification of a disagreeing pair (ARI < 0.99):** (a) *local optima*: the two selected log-likelihoods differ by more than 1 nat and the better one's partition also recovers the planted regimes or is the higher-likelihood one; (b) *recovery failure*: neither partition recovers the planted regimes (both ARI to truth < 0.6); (c) *labelling / ordering*: the partitions are the same (ARI >= 0.99) but the stable names differ (reported from the WP8 lineage when the same data are replayed; not computed in this diagnosis, listed only as the case that remains when (a) and (b) are excluded and ARI is high). The classification is descriptive; it chooses nothing.
- **What the diagnosis may decide:** the number of restarts and the ARI level that the consensus rule of Part 2 will declare. It may not decide an evaluation criterion. The levels of Part 2 are declared with their reasons before any development or evaluation fit is run, and the evaluation seeds are not touched by the diagnosis.

## 6. A1: the consensus rule (design, frozen by this commit)
**Rule C (FIT_UNSTABLE).** For one baseline fit, take the healthy restarts (CONVERGED and not collapsed) of the multi-start run, order them by log-likelihood, highest first (ties: lower restart index first) and take the best `m = 3`. The fit is **FIT_UNSTABLE** when (a) fewer than 3 healthy restarts exist, or (b) the smallest pairwise adjusted Rand index between the decoded day labels of those three restarts is below `tau = 0.99`. Otherwise the fit is stable. A fit that is not healthy (the selected restart is collapsed, or nothing converged) keeps its existing handling (withheld or error); it is not additionally labelled.
- **Why tau = 0.99, m = 3.** 0.99 is the level at which this package already calls two partitions "the same" (section 4); a consensus rule that tolerated less than the identity bar the criterion uses would accept disagreements it is meant to catch. The diagnosis's one failure at 0.9 and none at 0.95 or 0.99 is consistent with that, but with 12 to 17 pairs the choice between 0.95 and 0.99 is not resolved by data; I take the one that matches the declared definition of "same". m = 3 because two restarts agreeing can happen by chance in a flat likelihood (the exploratory rule "reached by at least 2" admitted one disagreement at both 10 and 20 restarts), three need not.
- **Restarts needed, and cost.** The rule needs no restart beyond those the production fit already runs: `fit_hssm` uses 10 restarts by default (hard minimum 10), the primary evaluation uses exactly that (the first 10 of the stored 20, which is the 10-restart run: the seeds are consecutive and the selected restart is bit-identical, tested). Cost of the rule: one ARI over three label vectors, negligible. The cost of more restarts is linear: 20 restarts cost twice the EM time of 10 (about 10 s versus 21 s per 150-day fit here). Whether 20 restarts is worth it is answered by the secondary evaluation (same fits, first 20 restarts) and reported; **the default stays 10**, and `n_initializations` remains a caller's choice. No adaptive escalation is implemented: an escalation that adds restarts to a flagged fit changes the selected model and therefore the raw outputs of a fit, which the bit-identity rule protects.
- **Implementation (additive).** New module `backbone/hssm/fit_stability.py` with a pure function over per-restart records; `fitting._restart_summary` adds the keys `consensus_m`, `consensus_tau`, `consensus_min_ari`, `n_healthy_restarts`, `fit_unstable`, `fit_unstable_reason` to `restart_summary` (existing keys unchanged); `lineage.FitSummary` gets the optional field `fit_unstable` (default `False`, i.e. unchanged behaviour when nothing is supplied) and `lineage.summarize_fit` reads it. In `lineage.step`, a fit with `fit_unstable` adds the reason `fit_unstable` and an anchor with `fit_unstable` adds `anchor_fit_unstable`: the state is `CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED` (never `ALIGNED`), the best guess is kept as for every other withheld claim, the message is the existing "cannot confirm this is the same as before". The first fit of a lineage is named as before and carries the flag; later alignments to a flagged anchor are not served, and the user choice (`NEW_NAMES`) works unchanged. Dual-engine fits carry `fit_unstable = None` (not assessed; dual lineage is never ALIGNED anyway, A6).
- **FIT_UNSTABLE is a lineage property, not a publishing property**: it does not withhold insights (`insights_withheld` is unchanged), it only blocks "same as before" claims. Whether an unstable fit should also change publishing is an owner decision, not made here.

## 7. A1: evaluation design (run once, after the freeze commit; seeds 9401..9448)
- Data: users 9401..9416 hard, 9417..9432 medium, 9433..9448 easy (the strata of section 3). Seed A: windows [0, t) for the weekly schedule t = 90, 97, ..., 146, 150, fit seed = user; seed B: the 14-day schedule t = 90, 104, 118, 132, 146, 150, fit seed = user + 1000 (these windows are a subset of the weekly ones, so seed A's 14-day lineage is replayed from its own fits). 20 restarts stored; the primary reading uses the first 10.
- Production lineage replay (`lineage.step` as committed, defaults of this commit) for each user and seed.
- **Primary criteria (n = 10 restarts):**
  1. **Identical lineage among served.** A case is a window of the 14-day schedule at which at least one of the two seeds' lineage ends `ALIGNED`; it is identical when both seeds end `ALIGNED` and the per-day stable ids over the window agree on at least 99% of days. Target: identical in at least 95% of cases. (This is stricter than "both served": a case where one seed serves and the other flags counts as not identical.) Also reported: the WP8 definition (cases fed by at least one seed, identical = same state and 99% agreement), to compare with the WP8 69.2%.
  2. **FIT_UNSTABLE is never ALIGNED:** zero `ALIGNED` steps whose new fit or anchor is flagged. Target: 0.
  3. **Served share** (accepted refits ending `ALIGNED` / accepted refits except the first of a stream, weekly schedule), pooled and per stratum: reported, **no threshold** (it depends on the separation of real users, which synthetic data cannot tell).
  4. **Bit-identity** of the baseline raw outputs of previously healthy fits against the WP8 final tree (7887118), battery of 12 fits as in WP8 Item 8: count identical; the `restart_summary` keys of WP8 unchanged, only the keys of section 6 added. Target: 12 of 12 (the BIC shift of WP8 does not apply because both trees are WP8 or later).
  5. **Mutation check:** at least 15 mutants of the consensus function and its use in `fitting` and `lineage`, all killed by the new tests.
- **Secondary (reported, no target):** the same criteria 1 to 3 with the first 20 restarts (the cost question); per stratum for every criterion; the share of fits flagged by stratum; the number of cases in which exactly one seed is flagged.
- Verdict wording as in section 2. Criterion 1 is judged "MET" only when the Wilson lower bound is at least 95%; with the numbers of cases expected (about 150 to 250 served in the medium and easy strata) a point estimate of 100% would give a lower bound near 98 to 99%.
- **What this cannot say:** the share of real users in each stratum, and the separation of real regimes. The evaluation says what the rule does when the planted separation is hard, medium or easy; it does not certify any real user.

## 8. A2: the identifiability gate (development set 9301..9360; selection rule frozen by this commit)
- **Development data:** users 9301..9320 hard, 9321..9340 medium, 9341..9360 easy (60 users), DEV-A: the weekly windows, fit seed = user, 10 restarts (600 fits, 540 alignments if all fits are accepted; at least 400 gate-eligible alignments are required: accepted fits with the same K as the anchor; fewer than 400 is reported and the rule is applied anyway). DEV-B: the 14-day windows, fit seed = user + 1000 (360 fits), used only to check rule C of section 6 on development data (a second look at the A1 rule before the evaluation; the rule is not tuned on it: a failure would be a dated change section, not an edit).
- **Candidates:** gate separation `h` in {0, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 5.0} (the existing `AlignmentConfig.min_separation`), crossed with a posterior-sharpness floor `s` in {0, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95} (both the new fit and the anchor must have a mean maximum posterior probability of at least `s`; `s = 0` is "off"). The consensus flag of rule C is part of every candidate (A1 makes it a production rule); candidates without it are reported in the curve only, to show what it contributes. The margin rule stays the WP8 baseline rule (ratio 0.5), unchanged.
- **Definitions.** Gate-eligible alignment: a lineage step of an accepted fit whose K equals the anchor's K (the WP8 alignment). Wrong-and-unflagged: an alignment that ends `ALIGNED` and is not strictly correct against the planted truth (the WP8 `alignment_correct`: both fits recover the planted regimes and the permutation is right). Coverage: alignments ending `ALIGNED` / all accepted refits except the first of a stream (WP8 criterion 2). The full coverage versus wrong-and-unflagged table over all candidates, pooled and per stratum, is reported.
- **Split.** Within each stratum the users with an even number are the calibration half (30 users), the others the validation half (30 users). Fixed now, never re-drawn.
- **Rule (frozen).** (1) On the calibration half keep the candidates with zero wrong-and-unflagged alignments; among them take the one with the highest coverage; ties go to the larger `h`, then the larger `s`. (2) Check it on the validation half: it is accepted if its wrong-and-unflagged share there is at most 1% (point estimate); otherwise (3) take, on the pooled development set, the candidate with zero wrong-and-unflagged and the highest coverage (same tie-breaks). If no candidate has zero wrong-and-unflagged on the half or the pooled set, the candidate with the fewest is taken and the number is reported. The chosen candidate becomes the new default of `AlignmentConfig` only if it differs from the current one (`h` = 4.0, `s` = 0); its numbers are in the report either way.
- **Why zero tolerance on a half and a 1% check on the other.** The 1% target of the owner cannot be certified by 0 wrong in a few hundred alignments (the Wilson upper bound of 0 of 300 is about 1.3%), so the rule does not pretend to: it picks the highest coverage that leaves nothing wrong on data it saw, then verifies on data it did not see; the evaluation of section 9 is the only test of the target.

# Part 3 (dated 2026-11-26, after the development fits were started and before any development or evaluation result was looked at): one change to section 6

## 11. Change: a flagged anchor is replaced by the first stable fit (replaces the sentence of section 6 "later alignments to a flagged anchor are not served")
**Why.** While implementing section 6 I saw, from the diagnosis alone (not from any development fit), that with the rule as written a lineage whose first fit (the 90-day window) is flagged can never be served again: the anchor stays that fit for ever, every later alignment carries `anchor_fit_unstable`, and the diagnosis shows that 90-day fits of medium-separation users are flagged in several cases (n = 10 restarts: 9205, 9206 and 9208 at 90 days). That would remove coverage for a reason that has nothing to do with whether later fits are trustworthy. The change is made before any development fit has been analysed and before any evaluation fit exists.
**What.** In `lineage.step`, when the anchor is flagged and the new fit (same K, matchable) is not flagged, the state of this step stays `CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED` (reason `anchor_fit_unstable`, the old names kept, no claim), but the new fit becomes the anchor, carrying the best-guess names of this step. Later stable fits are then compared with a stable anchor and can be `ALIGNED`. A flagged new fit never becomes an anchor this way; a stable anchor never moves on an unverified step (unchanged from WP8); a change of K is handled as before. Every `ALIGNED` step has a stable new fit and a stable anchor (tested as a property).
**What it changes in what I claim.** After the replacement the system says "same as before" between two stable fits; it does not say that the names before the replacement were right (that step is flagged and the user can choose new names, A3). The names at the replacement step come from matching an unstable anchor and are exactly as uncertain as that anchor; the evaluation scores that step as a flagged one (not served), and every later served step against the planted truth, so a wrong carry-over at the replacement would show up as wrong-and-unflagged later.
**Tests and mutants.** Five new tests (replacement, kept while the new fits are also flagged, the stable anchor does not move, a K change does not move it, best-guess names of a relabelled fit, and the property that no ALIGNED step involves a flagged fit or anchor) and four more mutants (29 in all).

## 13. Design
- **Data:** the WP7-R generator `integration_report/wp7r/scripts/k_select_study.py::planted(seed, K, design)` (easy: means up to 2.5 apart, noise 0.5; hard: 1.25 and 0.8; T = 75 rows, F = 7, z-scored; the planted structure is an assumption of the study, real separation is UNKNOWN). Planted K = 2 for the first 8 seeds of a range, 3 for the next 8, 4 for the last 8; both designs for every seed (48 datasets per range). **Development 9531..9554** (outside the ranges of the brief, unreserved, chosen by me), **evaluation 9501..9524** (once, after the freeze).
- **Fit:** the public path `fit_hssm(matrix, candidate_ks=(2, 3, 4, 5), n_initializations=10, max_iter=60, timestamps=days)`. Candidates go to 5 so that K = 4 has a K + 1 competitor; the production default `(2, 3, 4)` is not changed. The selection is BIC as before; `k_adequacy` is computed after it and cannot change it (tested, and the bit-identity battery covers the K-selection path).
- **Signals (module `backbone/hssm/k_adequacy.py`):** (1) `regimes_overlap`: the smallest pairwise distance between regime means in pooled within-regime sd, root mean square over features, below `d0`; (2) `k_not_clearly_best`: another candidate's BIC is within `delta` points of the selected one (the likelihood-gain test of K + 1 against its BIC penalty, expressed in BIC points); (3) `selected_k_is_largest_candidate` (K + 1 untried), switchable; (4) `restart_disagreement` (A1, FIT_UNSTABLE; a fit whose stability is not assessed counts as disagreeing). `k_unsure` is true when any enabled signal fires. The best guess stays shown; a developer alert (`alerts.emit`, user id hashed) is raised when `k_unsure`.
- **Ground truth for scoring:** a fit is **wrong-K** when the selected K differs from the planted K, **right-K** otherwise. Detection rate = flagged / wrong-K fits. False-alarm rate = flagged / right-K fits, reported separately for the easy design (where K is identifiable) and the hard design.
- **Development rule (frozen here):** grid `d0` in {0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0} x `delta` in {0, 3, 6, 10, 20} x signal (3) on or off (the other signals always on). Among grid points whose false-alarm rate on right-K fits of the **easy** design is at most 25% on the development set, take the one with the highest detection rate over all wrong-K fits (easy and hard together); ties: the lower false-alarm rate over all right-K fits, then the smaller `d0`, then the smaller `delta`, then signal (3) off. If no grid point meets the 25% cap, the one with the lowest easy false-alarm rate is taken and the shortfall reported. The chosen thresholds go into `backbone/hssm/config.py` before the evaluation (default values changed, which is allowed because the tag is new).
- **Evaluation levels (written now, with reasons), on seeds 9501..9524:** (a) **detection of wrong-K fits >= 60%**; (b) **false alarm on right-K fits of the easy design <= 25%**; (c) the hard design is reported with no level, because a right K on overlapping regimes is partly luck and a tag there is a reasonable answer. Reasons: the owner wants the tag to say "unsure" when the count is probably wrong without being on all the time; 60% and 25% are my judgement of a usable first version (more than half of the errors caught, at most one right answer in four tagged on clean data), not derived from anything. Wilson intervals are reported; wording "MET" / "MET on the point estimate, NOT DEMONSTRATED" as in section 2.
- **Also reported:** the share of all fits tagged by design and planted K; the reasons that fired; the BIC-selected K accuracy (to compare with WP7-R); how many wrong-K fits have no signal at all (the blind spot of section 12).
- **What this cannot say:** whether a real person's regime count is 3; it says how often these signals coincide with a wrong BIC choice on this generator.

## 15. B2 (run first; small)
- **Hypothesis (the cause is the data design, not the engine, the iterations or the seeds):** the share of publishable `shrink5` fits falls as the between-user spread grows.
- **Run:** seeds 9681..9692 (12), `generate_population_user(seed, 4, 2, PopulationSpec(), spread_factor=f)` for f in {0, 0.5, 1.0} (f = 0 is the Item 4 data; f = 1 is the Item 3 data; the script asserts that f = 0 reproduces `generate_synthetic_dual_timescale_data(n_segments=4)` exactly), the Item 3 call (stage B, 200 iterations, gate off for this study only), public path `fit_hssm`, `withheld` read before scoring. 36 fits.

Wilson intervals are reported; with 12 seeds per cell this is a screening size and the word is chosen by the rule, not by the interval. The reasons for withholding and the EM states (CONVERGED / REJECTED_UPDATE / MAX_ITER_REACHED) are tabulated by f.
- Engine state and iterations are not rerun: they are equal in the two studies (READ above); the final-engine Item 3 re-fit already showed that the engine moves the Item 3 numbers down, not up.

## 16. B1 design (module `backbone/hssm/cold_start_cert.py`, opt-in argument of `fit_hssm`)
- **Eligibility.** A fit is certified only when ALL hold: it started at a shared prior with `n0 > 0` (`used_shared_prior`); its EM state is `REJECTED_UPDATE` or `MAX_ITER_REACHED` (the EM rule already decides every converged fit); **every other withhold reason is absent** (collapsed regime, degenerate duration, degraded fit, Cholesky fallbacks in fit, in the states pass or later all still withhold, and certification never overrides them); T >= 24 rows. Anything else: `NOT_CERTIFIED` with the reason, or `NOT_APPLICABLE` for a converged fit or a fit without a prior.
- **Rolling-origin held-out score.** Origins at 50%, 65% and 80% of the T rows (o_j = ceil(f_j T)); the model is **refitted with the same procedure** (same class, prior, n0, seed, `max_iter`, Dmax, K, F, L) on rows [0, o_j) and scored one step ahead (forward filter with frozen parameters, as `predictive_increments`) on the block [o_j, o_(j+1)) (o_4 = T), against the single Gaussian (mean and covariance, ddof 1) fitted on the same rows [0, o_j). The pooled per-step differences d_t (model minus Gaussian) are in time order. Requirements: every block has at least 3 rows; o_1 >= 12.
- **Statistic.** mean(d) and its standard error by a Bartlett-kernel HAC estimator with bandwidth ceil(n / 4) (capped at n - 1; regimes persist about 15 steps, so a short bandwidth would understate the error), `lower = mean(d) - z * se`. Certified requires `lower > 0` and all increments finite.
- **Parameter stability (the brief's second condition).** The regime observation means of the full fit and of the fit at the last origin (80%), regimes matched by the permutation of least distance, must differ by a root mean square of at most `TAU = 0.25` in units of each feature's single-Gaussian standard deviation on the full T. `TAU` is fixed here, not tuned: a quarter of a marginal standard deviation is the size of movement I would not call "settled"; the share of fits that fail only this condition is reported so that its effect is visible.
- **Fail closed.** An exception, a non-finite increment or mean, a refit that did not finish or was degraded, too few rows, an ineligible fit: not certified, the reason recorded. The state is a separate field (`HSSMResult.certification["state"]` in {`CERTIFIED_PRIOR_ANCHORED`, `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`}); `fit_state` keeps what EM said. `withheld_reason` drops **only** the `fit_state_not_converged` reason, **only** when certified. Default of the new argument `fit_hssm(certify_prior_anchored=False)`: nothing changes for any existing call; the product layer opts in (open owner decision).
- **Margin `z` (development rule, frozen here).** Development seeds 9631..9642 (12 users, 3 lengths), arms `shrink5`, `scrambled` (the prior's observation parameters `d_obs`, `C_obs`, `R_obs` with the feature axis permuted by the cyclic shift f -> f + 1, and the regime means of the duration parameters swapped), `shifted` (every `d_obs` + 1.5), `personal` (no prior, n0 = 0: not eligible in production, the same function is applied to it only as a test of the test). Per-fit rolling-origin increments are stored, so `z` is applied offline. `z` is the smallest value of {1, 2, 3} for which the controls pooled over the three arms and three lengths certify in at most 10% of their eligible fits **and** at most half the pooled certified share of `shrink5`; if none qualifies, `z = 3` and the shortfall is reported. Control validity check on the development seeds: the held-out score of the shared-only model under each control prior is at least 1 nat per step worse than under the true prior (mean over seeds); a control that fails the check is reported as not a valid control.
- **Evaluation (once, after the freeze file), seeds 9601..9624, lengths 30 / 60 / 120 (segments 2 / 4 / 8), 24 users, nothing excluded, stage B settings (`max_iter` 200), arms `shrink5`, `scrambled`, `shifted`, `personal`, plus the shared-only and single-Gaussian scores.** Reported, per length: (E1) the share of `shrink5` fits publishable under the EM rule, certified, and publishable after (EM-publishable or certified), among all fits and among eligible fits; (E2) the held-out gain over the single Gaussian on the **certified** fits (mean, paired se over seeds, `mean - 3 se`, seeds favouring; judged only for n >= 8, as in WP7-R); (E3) the same on all **publishable-after** fits, and the sentence of WP7-R item 3: "survives" only if every length is judged and every `mean - 3 se > 0`; (E4) risk-coverage (a withheld fit means the single Gaussian is served): coverage, gain on all 24 users, `mean - 3 se`; "net gain shown" only if every length has `mean - 3 se > 0`; (E5) the controls: certified share per arm and length, MET when pooled over lengths each control certifies in at most half of the pooled `shrink5` certified share and at most 10% of its eligible fits; the held-out gain of any certified control fit is shown; (E6) the certificate's precision: the share of certified fits (all arms) whose held-out gain over the single Gaussian is positive, with the Wilson interval. No level beyond the sentences above is declared for E1 and E6: they are reported so that the reader can judge.
- **What this cannot say:** the generator is the WP5 population (centred on the prior's generator: favourable), K = 2, F = 4, L = 2, Dmax 25; real people may sit further from any prior. A certified fit means "predicted better than a single Gaussian out of sample on these rolling origins", not "the regimes are right".


# Part 6 (dated 2026-11-26, before any A4 fit): A4, a negative control that can fail

## 17. What I had seen
- READ: WP8 control (a) (z-space without the stored scaler) was **inert** (`tests/WP8_ITEM8_RESULTS.md`): the windows grow from day 0, so each window's mean and sd change slowly and the z-space basis barely moves. A monotone rescaling also keeps the order of the regimes, so a mapping by nearest distance survives it; the control can only fail where the rescaling is different in different features and large enough to change which regime is nearest.
- READ: the generator (`data/synthetic_identity.py`) and the generator option added for A4 (`feature_scale_change`, default off, 8 default stream hashes pinned in `tests/test_wp9_a4_generator.py`, RUN equal to the WP8 file). No A4 seed has been fitted. Development seeds 9261..9276 (16 users) and evaluation seeds 9281..9296 (16 users) are mine and unreserved.

## 18. A4 design
- **Protocol S (fresh windows, not the production growing window):** 150-day streams, stratum easy (planted separation 1.6 to 3.0; the control needs a method that works on the unperturbed stream), K = 3, windows of 45 days `[end - 45, end)` for end = 45, 60, ..., 150 (eight windows per user; the first is the anchor), 10 restarts, the production selection and publish rule (`wp9_fit_lib`), 10 restarts, the lineage `step` with the frozen defaults at the time of the run. The planted change: `feature_scale_change = dict(features, factor, start = 60)`: the within-regime noise of the listed features is multiplied by `factor` from day 60, the regime means do not move. Raw-unit regime identity is therefore unchanged, while the within-window scale of those features is not.
- **Why fresh windows:** with windows that start at day 0 the change would be diluted by the earlier days (that is the WP8 result). Whether the product ever refits on fresh windows is not known to me; this study tests whether the stored scaler is what keeps the identity when the windows differ, which is the question of the control.
- **Method pair (the same fits, replayed twice):** (R) the production matching, raw means divided by the **anchor's stored scaler**; (Z) the WP8 control (a), each fit compared in its own z-space (`backbone.hssm.lineage._vectors` patched exactly as `item8_analyze.patch_zspace`).
- **Development rule (frozen here), seeds 9261..9276:** three designs: (A) features (5, 6) factor 0.1, (B) features (3, 4, 5, 6) factor 0.1, (C) features (0, 1, 2) factor 4.0. A design qualifies when, on the development fits, (R) has correct among served >= 95% and wrong-and-unflagged <= 1% over at least 40 alignments **and** (Z) has correct among served < 95% or wrong-and-unflagged > 1%. Among qualifying designs take the first in the order A, B, C. If none qualifies, the control **stays inert on every design I tried**, that is the result, no design is searched further, and the evaluation is still run once on design A so that the statement is measured.
- **Evaluation (once, seeds 9281..9296, the chosen design), levels written now:** (R) correct among served >= 95% and wrong-and-unflagged <= 1% over at least 40 alignments ("the stream is one where the method works"); (Z) **fails as required** iff correct among served < 95% or wrong-and-unflagged > 1%; otherwise **inert**, reported as inert and not as passed. Also reported: coverage of both, the paired counts (alignments served wrongly by Z and rightly by R, and the reverse), the number of fits that the publish rule withheld, and the same two methods on the same users without the scale change (so the effect of the change is visible).
- **What this cannot say:** that real streams have such scale changes. It shows, on a planted one, that the control can fail and that the stored scaler is what prevents it.


# Part 7 (dated 2026-11-26, before any A3 fit): A3, K-change detection and candidates

## 19. What I had seen
- READ: WP8 criterion 6 (`tests/WP8_ITEM8_RESULTS.md`, `item8_analyze.crit_6`): K selection on the growing window [0, t) saw the change in 3 of 24 streams; candidate precision 3 of 38 (7.9%); 38 of 38 refits whose fitted K differed from the anchor's were `K_CHANGED` with no "same as before". The 38 K-differing refits include BIC noise on the pre-change window, so the precision number mixes real and spurious K differences.
- READ the generator: a **split** turns about half of the later parent-regime segments (from day 90) into a child regime whose mean is the parent's plus 2 within-regime sd along a fixed direction; a **merge** replaces the later days of regime b by the mean of regime a (day 90). I expect, before any fit, that a merge is **not distinguishable from an unvisited regime** in a 45-day window (the regimes are visited in turn, a regime is absent from 45 days with a probability of the order of one in five), so merge detection will be close to its own false-alarm rate; this is why no level is written for merges.
- No A3 seed has been fitted. Development seeds 9221..9232 and evaluation seeds 9241..9252 (12 users each, mine, unreserved).

## 20. Design
- **Signal (module `backbone/hssm/k_change.py`, pure functions plus a windowed fit):** at a refit with the anchor's K_a, the **recent window** R = the last 45 days of the new window is fitted at K' in {K_a - 1, K_a, K_a + 1} (10 restarts, production selection and publish rule) and compared by BIC. `k_change_suspected` in direction `up` (K' = K_a + 1) or `down` (K_a - 1) when that K' has the lowest BIC and beats the K_a fit on R by at least `delta` BIC points. Every other case: no signal. A fit of R that does not finish or is not accepted: no signal (fail closed in the sense that nothing is claimed; the lineage is not served as ALIGNED on that account either, see below).
- **Lineage use (additive):** `FitSummary.k_change` (default None = nothing known). With a suspected change and the SAME fitted K the step becomes `CONTINUITY_UNVERIFIED` with the reason `k_change_suspected` and the old names are kept ("cannot confirm this is the same as before"; `may_claim_same_as_before` stays False); with a different fitted K the step is `K_CHANGED` as before. A stable anchor does not move on such a step.
- **Candidates from posterior overlap (replaces nothing; the old nearest-mean rule stays and is measured next to it):** every row of the recent window is assigned to the anchor regime with the highest Gaussian density (anchor means and the within-regime variances of its own window, raw units); for each regime j of the recent fit the share of its rows that belong to each old regime is computed. **Split candidate** (`up`): an old regime that is the dominant parent (>= 50% of the rows) of two or more regimes of the recent fit. **Merge candidate** (`down`): a regime of the recent fit whose rows divide between two old regimes with at least 25% each, the pair of the two largest shares.
- **Protocol K (study):** 12 users per stage, three streams each (split at day 90, merge at day 90, no change; default WP8 generator, planted K = 3), anchor = the 10-restart K = 3 fit of [0, 90), refits at day 118, 134 and 150 with R = [t - 45, t); fit seed = user. The WP8 comparison is the K selection (candidates 2, 3, 4) on the final cumulative window [0, 150), the WP8 definition of "sees the change".
- **Development rule (frozen here), seeds 9221..9232:** per direction the smallest `delta` in {0, 3, 6, 10, 20, 40} whose false-alarm rate on the no-change streams (a stream with at least one signal of that direction at any of the three refits) is at most 10% on the development set; if none, the largest and the shortfall is reported. The two thresholds go into `backbone/hssm/config.py` before the evaluation.
- **Evaluation levels (once, seeds 9241..9252, written now, with reasons):** (a) **split detection >= 50%**: a stream counts when an `up` signal occurs at some refit; a child segment appears in about half of the later parent segments, so about half of the split streams contain enough child days by day 150 and a detector cannot be expected above that; (b) **false alarm <= 10% per direction** on the no-change streams: a tag that is on for one stable user in five would be noise; (c) **split-candidate precision >= 80%** among the `up` signals of the split streams (WP8's expectation, now that spurious K differences are not counted: the candidate is the parent when the child is found); merge detection, merge-candidate precision and the old nearest-mean candidate precision on the same signals are REPORTED without a level; (d) **`k_change_suspected` and `K_CHANGED` records never claim "same as before": 100%** of the records of the study (also a unit test). Wilson intervals; "MET" only when the interval supports the level, otherwise "MET on the point estimate, NOT DEMONSTRATED" (12 streams per kind is a small sample and several of these will be the second word). Reported next to WP8: 3/24 detection and 7.9% precision.
- **What this cannot say:** that a real change of the number of regimes looks like these two planted changes.
